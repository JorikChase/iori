## What was not found

- I found no open-licence SEM or histology image of a crypt trabecula in cross-section.
- I found no paper that measures trabeculae in µm.
- Tan et al. 2025 (IOVS 66(5):36, CC BY-NC-ND) gives an ABL thickness of about 40 µm, ABL pores of 10–50 µm (citing Freddo), and crypt areas of 0.015–0.3 mm². Its donor-iris photographs (Fig. 4) show "sparse stroma fibres, mainly collagen strands and blood vessels". The licence does not allow copying them here.
- The following are textbook statements from memory (Hogan, Alvarado & Weddell; Rohen), not checked against a source this session:
  - the trabeculae inside crypts are radial vessels in thick collagen sleeves, plus loose collagen bundles;
  - stroma collagen fibrils are about 60 nm thick and gather into bundles;
  - vessels run radially and wavy, so they can follow pupil movement.

## Measurements from the photo (eye in the Commons picture, ±15%)

| feature | value |
|---|---|
| bridge (rung) thickness in mid-span | **40–60 µm** (5–7 px) |
| bridge at its ends, where it joins the rim | **80–120 µm**: flares into a fillet, no hard joint |
| fine fibres inside a collarette bundle (C) | **≤ 20–25 µm**, at the resolution limit |
| fibres per collarette bundle | **3–6 visible**; probably more, finer |
| spacing of rungs in a crypt ladder (A) | **150–250 µm** |
| large crypt | about 1 mm across. Its arched trabecula follows the rim, about 50 µm thick |
| crypts between collarette bundles | lens-shaped, radially long, about 100–300 µm |

## Shape observations

1. **A bridge is a rope, not a sheet.** Mid-span, the cross-section is round. The strands converge into a single rope of 40–60 µm.
2. **Thinnest in the middle, flared at both ends.** At the rim the rope widens to about 2× and fans into the sheet with a smooth fillet. Single fibres peel off along the rim (crop C).
3. **Branching.** Y and T junctions are common: a bridge splits in two, or a second bridge leaves from its middle. Near the collarette the bridges form a net of polygonal holes (crop A, top), not parallel bars.
4. **Fibres inside a bundle (crop C).** The fibres run nearly parallel, with a gentle twist or weave. Some cross diagonally from one bundle to the next. The surface shows fine lengthwise grain.
5. **Course.** Bridges are slightly wavy, not ruler-straight. None sags visibly at this scale.

## How eye 26 is drawn today, compared to the reference

Each band between crypts is an even slab of parallel tubes:

- up to three layers of tubes;
- each tube r 9 µm (`F.tubeB`), spaced 23 µm apart (the fibre wavelength);
- every layer straight along the band, the same all the way across.

So a wide band reads as a flat ribbon of parallel tubes. In the reference it would be a round rope that flares at its ends.
