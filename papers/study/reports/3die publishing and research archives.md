# Turn one paper tree into a press

iori's papers are already one connected body, but it is a **tree rooted at paper 0, wired in only one direction**. Paper 0 (fractals.html) links out to every other paper. Each sub-paper (0.1 Metafractal, 0.2 Radiance Fields, 0.3 Silviculture) links back only through its byline and has no sibling navigation. Paper 0.3 also imports paper 0's code and corrects it. Six of the nine texts are hidden, so paper 0 is the only public way in. The cheapest honest way to show this is not a global graph. It is a generated "series" block in every paper plus one plain-HTML archive page, built from typed `uses` fields in `pages.meta.json`, with link previews and an optional small build-time diagram (number on one axis, date on the other). "3die publishing" has a real precedent: **Shelter Press**, a French house that issues records and books under one name with a numbered series. Today, though, the imprint would be almost entirely iori's work, because the other label artists have almost no text in the repo. No surveyed archive page handles decimal sub-numbering or live-figure status, so the research favours designing our own on the plain outline pattern of Bret Victor and Robin Sloan. Real citability is nearly free: Highwire meta tags, Zenodo DOIs, Software Heritage, WACZ snapshots and FAIR signposting cost nothing. Czech ISSN and ISBN are probably free but unconfirmed. Crossref, at about 200 USD a year plus 1 USD per paper, is the only paid step worth weighing. The worry that internal markdown was publicly reachable is settled: four sample URLs on iori.me and 3die.fr returned 404 on 2026-11-09, and the deploy excludes `*.md`, `*.py` and `tools/` ([server_setup.sh](../../../server_setup.sh)).

## Paper 0 spawned five texts and still carries every link

The registry in `papers/series.py` lists eight numbered entries. Four are written papers: 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3. Three are stubs (1 Cellular Automata, 2 Tensor Landscapes, 3 Document Diffusion), and paper 4 is the older Lambda Cognition essay. A ninth long text, **The Fractal Engine, Tool by Tool** (fractal-engine/paper.html, about 16,900 words), uses the same stylesheet and reference tooling but sits outside the registry ([papers/series.py](../../../papers/series.py)). By size the "0 family" dominates. Paper 0 runs about 8,200 words with 79 references, 0.3 about 9,400 words with 42, 0.1 about 3,500 and 0.2 about 3,200. The stubs are 640 to 700 words each, and paper 4 about 2,000. These are word counts of visible text, accurate to about ±10%. **Only paper 0, the engine paper and paper 4 are public.** Every sub-paper, every stub and the series index are `hidden` ([pages.meta.json](../../../pages.meta.json)).

Conceptually every other paper is a part of paper 0 "made a paper". 0.1 comes from §1.6, 0.3 from §3.1–3.2, paper 2 from chapter 6, paper 3 from chapter 5 and paper 1 from §8.1. 0.2 takes apart the engine's object and calls itself a companion to 0 and 0.1 ([fractals.html](../../../fractals.html), [radiance-fields.html](../../../radiance-fields.html)). The series is therefore **a tree, not the chain 0→1→2→3→4** that the index copy implies. The 0.x branch grew in October and November 2026, while papers 1–3 have stayed stubs since 12 October. Only paper 0.3 has a dependency running both ways. It imports paper 0's simulation and terrain modules (`silviculture-core.js`, `landscape-terrain.js`, `landscape-stats.js`) "by import, never fork". It also corrected paper 0's claim about Makowski's −3/2 law, and its chapter 5.2 is a ranked to-do list for paper 0 ([papers/silviculture/PLAN.md](../../../papers/silviculture/PLAN.md)). Yet 0.3 mentions "paper 0" about 46 times and hyperlinks it only once, in the byline.

The **wiring is asymmetric** in four ways. First, the series list exists twice. `series.py` generates it into the stubs and the index, and paper 0 §8.2 holds a hand-written copy with different descriptions. A whole commit (a18d9b1) exists only to refresh paper 0's lastmod after that list changed. Second, the sub-papers carry no series list and no site menu, because hidden pages get no burger. Third, references are numbered per page by `refs.py`, so 20 entries are duplicated across five shared pairs: 8 between paper 0 and 0.3, 8 between 0.1 and the engine paper, 3 between 0.2 and the engine paper, and 1 between paper 0 and 0.1. Fourth, Petri, Flame Strains, Iris Engine and the Fractal Engine app are cited by paper 0 but none of them links back to any paper ([papers/fractals/refs.py](../../../papers/fractals/refs.py)). Several small inconsistencies would leak into any archive page:

- The index subtitle still says "five papers" while it lists eight entries.
- Every stub shares one hard-coded "first published 12 October 2026" footer.
- "First published" means first commit for one paper, first deploy for another, and the oldest ancestor for paper 0.
- Sitemap `lastmod` follows only the HTML file, so code-only revisions to a paper never register ([site.py](../../../site.py)).

### What the interface research says to show

The research on linked writing points clearly away from a global graph. The one large controlled result comes from Wikipedia's **Page Previews**. In the 2017–18 A/B tests, readers opened more distinct pages and pressed back less, and only about 0.01% turned previews off ([MediaWiki: Page Previews](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews)). gwern.net shows the full pattern without any graph: popups, backlinks shown in their surrounding context, and lazy transclusion, all generated offline ([gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design)). Global graph views are widely judged pretty but unused. In one self-selected Obsidian poll, 178 of 302 voters never navigate with the global graph ([r/ObsidianMD poll](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/ObsidianMD/comments/1hg9qr3/how_often_do_you_use_the_global_graph_in_obsidian)). That critique targets hairballs of hundreds of untyped notes, though. For graphs of 20–50 nodes, node-link diagrams beat matrices on path-following ([Network Science experiment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/network-science/article/understanding-nodelink-and-matrix-visualizations-of-networks-a-largescale-online-experiment/C293136C4761F09D4C2C328E2BC19287)). "How does 0.3 get back to paper 0?" is exactly a path question.

The closest structural model is the **Lean blueprint**. Its dependency graph is generated from explicit `\uses{}` declarations, nodes are coloured by status, and the linear document stays the main view ([leanblueprint](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/leanblueprint); [Tao's PFR graph](https://teorth.github.io/pfr/blueprint/dep_graph_document.html)). Applied here, each paper's entry in `pages.meta.json` would gain `series`, `number`, `parent`, `status` and a `uses` list of typed edges:

- *extends*
- *reuses code*
- *reuses figure*
- *cites*
- *corrects*

These map loosely onto CiTO terms, but that mapping still has to be checked against the spec ([CiTO](https://sparontologies.github.io/cito/current/cito.html)). `site.py` would then render a marked `SERIES:BEGIN/END` block into every paper, as it already does for the NAV and MENU regions. The block would list the parent, children and backlinks with section anchors, each saying what kind of link it is ("0.3 §2 reuses paper 0's drainage instrument"). Scite's lesson is that the citing sentence, not a coloured edge, carries the meaning ([CASRAI: scite](https://casrai.org/research-tools/ai-research-assistants/scite)). Nelson's split between links and transclusion fits the shared modules. Code or a figure that 0.3 imports from paper 0 is an identical part, so it deserves a visible "from paper 0 §x" source strip ([Xanadu vision](https://xanadu.com/xuvision.html)). The iori.me landing's WebGL node web already plays the "pretty global graph" role, so the archive can do the plainer job.

## A label imprint today means iori's papers plus a promise

The text body is lopsided. The paper series and the engine paper come to roughly **45,000 words, all by iori**. The other public prose is small: the Audio Tools docs (hidden, about 1,400 words), Lambda Cognition, a Czech childhood memoir inside the hidden timeline (28 text entries, about 1,200 words), and Lukáš Kalivoda's exhibition texts. Those Kalivoda texts are a hidden draft assembled from public sources and still awaiting his review ([kalivoda/index.html](../../../kalivoda/index.html)). jáchym, the only other label artist described as working "in text", has a one-paragraph bio. Whatever he and moises have posted lives in the VPS `posts` table, not in git ([jachym.html](../../../jachym.html); [api/main.py](../../../api/main.py)). **Liner notes, release texts, interviews and reviews do not exist anywhere searched.** The release plan holds metadata only ([tools/release/PLAN.md](../../../tools/release/PLAN.md)). Behind the public texts sit about 500,000 words of internal studies, specs and briefs (Iris, Petri, MOSHINE, the fractal-engine DESIGN.md). They are working documents, not publications, and none of them is web-reachable.

Three structural facts constrain any imprint. First, the papers live on **iori.me**, but their byline already reads "3DIE — Design, Art & Technologies Label", while the label lives on **3die.fr** ([papers/series.py](../../../papers/series.py)). Second, the registry has no text type: the papers are scattered between the `essay` and `fractals` categories, and `site.py` emits no Article or ScholarlyArticle JSON-LD ([site.py](../../../site.py)). Third, the label-wide Organization `sameAs` includes soundcloud.com/5gmaelstroem, which is crow_archduke's SoundCloud, so the label's machine identity is tangled with one artist's ([index.html](../../../index.html)).

### Shelter Press is the model; Zer0 Books is the warning

Among labels and collectives that publish text, **Shelter Press** fits 3die best. It is a French independent house run by Bartolomé Sanson and Félicia Atkinson that issues records and books under one name. Its bilingual multi-author *Spectres* book series reached volume III in 2021, and its books are distributed by Les presses du réel ([Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/shelter-press-label-profile); [Bleep: Spectres III](https://bleep.com/merch/256599-various-artists-spectres-iii-ghosts-in-the-machine); [Les presses du réel](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/editeur.php?id=155)). **Primary Information** adds the economic model: website PDFs are "always free", next to e-books, paper books and handmade editions, an approach it calls "degrees of free" ([Artforum roundtable](https://www.artforum.com/columns/the-year-in-independent-art-publishing-a-roundtable-222530/)). That maps directly onto open web papers with optional printed objects. **Blank Forms** numbers its anthologies and its art editions in separate sequences, which shows how one house can run several series side by side ([Artbook D.A.P.](https://www.artbook.com/blank-forms.html)). In Prague, **tranzit.cz** runs an active publishing house with yearly editorial plans for 2025 and 2026, which makes it a local peer and possible partner ([tranzit editorial plan 2026](https://cz.tranzit.org/en/publications/0/publication/editorial-plan-2026)).

The cautionary tale is **Zer0 Books**. Its founders started it in 2009 as an imprint inside John Hunt Publishing, left in 2014 after disputes, and lost the name ([Wikipedia: Repeater Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeater_Books)). 3die publishing should therefore stay inside 3die's own entity and keep its name and rights. **Distill** gives the second warning. It was the closest precedent for interactive web papers, and it went on hiatus in July 2021 from volunteer burnout. Its editors told authors to self-publish with the template rather than wait for a journal ([Distill Hiatus](https://distill.pub/2021/distill-hiatus/)). A house imprint with light editorial review is sustainable at 3die's size. A peer-reviewed venue is not. Small Press Distribution, once the default for small presses, **closed in March 2024** ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/94690-small-press-distribution-shuts-down.html)). If print ever matters, the realistic channels are print on demand (Bookvault for the UK and EU) and fairs. The GHMP Art Book Fair in Prague is local, held in September, and open to fanzine makers ([GHMP ABF 26](https://www.ghmp.cz/en/accompanying-events/ghmp-art-book-fair-26/)). Offprint Paris and Miss Read Berlin follow ([offprint.org](https://offprint.org); [e-flux: Miss Read 2026](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/6787045/miss-read-2026)).

For licensing, the open-access presses converge on authors keeping copyright under a non-exclusive licence. punctum uses CC BY-NC-SA by default ([punctum contract](https://docs.punctumbooks.com/books/about-our-book-production-process/page/contract)). meson prefers CC BY-SA, and its co-founder argues that NC licences are not really open ([meson OA statement](https://meson.press/we-are-committed-to-open-access/)). Interactive works split the licence: CC for text and figures, an OSI-approved licence for code, which is what JOSS requires ([JOSS review criteria](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/review_criteria.html)). How CC licences interact with Czech and French moral rights, which cannot be waived, was not researched.

## No surveyed archive handles decimal series or live figures

A live survey of 21 research-archive and index pages found **five shapes**:

1. A single reverse-chronological list: Distill, Ink & Switch, Craig Mod, arXiv.
2. An outline of headings, each holding a list: Bret Victor, Robin Sloan, gwern, UbuWeb.
3. Numbered issues: Asterisk, Works in Progress, e-flux, Kernel.
4. A card grid with filters: Maggie Appleton, Anthropic, Sakana.
5. No index at all: Andy Matuschak's notes.

The design-led archives are mostly shapes 1 and 2, and they keep very little per entry. Bret Victor groups work under short labels, then by year, with one descriptor per item and secondary formats written inline as words ("talk, notes") ([worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/)). Ink & Switch shows a title, one line, a type and a year ([inkandswitch.com/essay](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/)). Numbering exists only as magazine issue numbers (Asterisk 01–16), running story numbers (The Pudding #228) or arXiv IDs ([asteriskmag.com/issues](https://asteriskmag.com/issues); [pudding.cool/archives](https://pudding.cool/archives/)). **No site surveyed uses decimal sub-numbering**, and none marks on its index that an entry contains live figures or needs WebGPU. gwern is the only strong case for per-page status and revision metadata: created and modified dates, a status word, confidence and a site changelog ([gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design)). Distill remains the reference for article-level citation infrastructure: an ISSN, a Crossref DOI prefix, Scholar indexing, and public review on GitHub ([distill.pub/journal](https://distill.pub/journal/)).

The anti-patterns are consistent across the survey:

- promotional blocks inside the list (Pudding);
- unexplained symbols (Sloan's ★);
- relative dates that go stale (Appleton's "about 1 year ago");
- a landing page that shows only the current issue, with no visible back index (e-flux);
- dead archive URLs (Works in Progress `/issues/` now returns 404).

Brutalist Web Design's rules give a usable checklist: underlined links, a working back button, scrolling instead of pagination, and speed treated as a feature ([brutalist-web.design](https://brutalist-web.design/)). An archive of 10–50 entries needs no filters, pagination or JavaScript.

The verdict is to **design our own, on the Victor/Sloan outline pattern**. Adopting any one site's system would mean dropping the tree structure and the interactivity status, which are the two things this series has that others do not. The repo already has two visual poles to draw on. The 3die.fr label style is black, Courier New, uppercase letter-spaced labels and full-width rows with 1px rules ([jachym.html](../../../jachym.html)). The papers' light reading sheet uses Lora and Inter, a 40rem column and semantic figure colours ([papers/fractals/paper.css](../../../papers/fractals/paper.css)). A natural split is a brutalist index on 3die.fr and light reading pages wherever the papers live. Each entry would carry a number, title, one-line descriptor, a short date, a status word, and formats written as words (web · pdf · code · data · live figures). Indentation would carry the 0 → 0.x tree. The richer metadata (citation block, revision history, errata) belongs on the paper page, not the index. This is where gwern and Distill both put it.

## Free identifiers cover most of the need; Crossref is the one paid choice

Google Scholar will index HTML papers on a personal or custom site if four conditions hold ([Google Scholar inclusion](https://scholar.google.com/intl/en/scholar/inclusion.html)):

- each paper has its own URL;
- the full text or abstract is visible without clicks or scrolling;
- the page carries `citation_title`, `citation_author` and `citation_publication_date` meta tags;
- the paper can be reached through plain HTML links, in files of at most **5 MB**.

These tags slot directly into the generated `SEO:BEGIN/END` block. Alongside them, `site.py` could emit schema.org `ScholarlyArticle` with `isPartOf` a `CreativeWorkSeries`, and FAIR Signposting `<link>`s (`cite-as` the DOI, `author` the ORCID, `license`, `describedby`) ([FAIR Signposting](https://signposting.org/FAIR/)). The heavy, JS-driven papers need a static abstract at the top and a PDF snapshot for `citation_pdf_url`.

For DOIs, **Zenodo** is the free, realistic route for an individual. Each version gets its own DOI, and a concept DOI resolves to the latest version. Records hold up to 50 GB and are retained "for the lifetime of the repository". GitHub releases are archived automatically and mirrored to Software Heritage ([Zenodo policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/); [Zenodo versioning](https://zenodo.org/help/versioning); [Zenodo GitHub docs](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/github/)). A single repo release would give the whole repo one DOI, so the better fit is one Zenodo upload record per paper. Each record would hold a PDF, a WACZ capture of the live page and a source zip, and would be linked to the others through `isPartOf`, optionally inside a "3die publishing" community. Zenodo stores files but will not run the live page, so its landing page must link back to the canonical URL.

**Crossref** would make 3die the owner of its own DOI prefix, with DOIs resolving to 3die.fr. It costs **200 USD a year at the lowest tier plus 1 USD per current article record** in the 2026 schedule, roughly 4,400–4,600 CZK a year at a few papers ([Crossref fees](https://www.crossref.org/fees/)). Its documentation speaks only of "organisations", though, so whether a sole trader qualifies is unconfirmed. A sponsor route exists but no Czech or French sponsor was identified ([Crossref sponsors](https://www.crossref.org/membership/about-sponsors/)). **arXiv** is a poor fit: it would freeze the figures into images, and new submitters need endorsement ([arXiv submit help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/submit/index.html)).

National identifiers follow the **publisher's country, not the domain**. A Prague-based publisher applies to the Czech centres at the National Library (NK ČR), even with a .fr domain.

**ISSN.** An online series can get an ISSN if it has editorial content, a stable title, a valid URL, a precise subject, and named editorial responsibility, which is "generally more than one person". ISSN rules also exclude "personal content, such as a personal website" ([ISSN for electronic media](https://www.issn.org/understanding-the-issn/assignment-rules/the-issn-for-electronic-media/)). A series published under iori.me with a single editor is exactly what that clause catches. A titled series on its own index page, with a publisher statement naming 3die publishing and ideally a second person in an editorial role, is what passes.

**ISBN.** The Czech ISBN agency assigns numbers free, including to individuals, but only for a *finished* online publication, never for a web page as a whole ([Ikaros: ISBN a online publikace](https://ikaros.cz/print/13593)). A living page would therefore need a frozen, versioned edition to carry an ISBN. Treat "free" as unverified: the source dates from 2011, and the NK ČR pages could not be fetched. France's AFNIL serves francophone publishers and charges 37 EUR plus VAT per first request ([AFNIL tarification](https://www.afnil.org/tarification/)), so it is not the route for a Czech resident.

**Legal deposit.** No mandatory deposit of online works applies to this case in either country. Being archived is voluntary: nominate the sites to Webarchiv, whose yearly broad crawl covers only .cz domains, and to BnF's "Signaler un site web" ([BnF: Dépôt légal du web](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/depot-legal-du-web); [NK ČR Webarchive](https://nkp.cz/en/about-us/organizational-structure/webarchive-department)).

### Preserving a WebGPU page takes a stack, not a service

No single service reliably preserves a live WebGPU page. The free stack has four layers:

- **Code:** Software Heritage (SWHID is ISO/IEC 18670), which Zenodo fills automatically ([SWH](https://softwareheritage.org/how-to-archive-reference-code)).
- **Live page:** a self-made **WACZ** from Webrecorder, captured while actually exercising every figure so that workers, shaders and data files are recorded ([WACZ 1.1.1](https://specs.webrecorder.net/wacz/1.1.1/)).
- **Public web archive:** a Wayback Machine Save Page Now on each publish, which is known to be weak on JS-heavy pages ([Internet Archive blog](https://blog.archive.org/2019/10/23/the-wayback-machines-save-page-now-is-new-and-improved/)).
- **Long-term fallback:** videos and a still-image PDF of each figure, deposited with the WACZ, because replay still needs a WebGPU-capable browser. This mirrors Rhizome's mix of web archives and emulation ([Rhizome almanac](https://almanac.rhizome.org/pages/artifacts-infrastructure)).

Perma.cc now costs individuals from 10 USD a month ([Perma.cc accounts](https://perma.cc/docs/accounts)). LOCKSS, CLOCKSS and Portico are journal-publisher services at the wrong scale.

Versioning has one repo-specific trap. Crossref advises separate, linked correction notices instead of silent in-place edits ([Crossref](https://www.crossref.org/publications/connected-dynamic-scholarly-record/)), and Zenodo never modifies a published version. Paper 0.3, however, imports live modules from `papers/fractals/`. A "frozen" version of 0.3 would silently change whenever paper 0's code changes, unless each version folder vendors its imports or loads them from a commit-pinned path. The footer convention already in place ("first published <date> · <commit>") is the right seed for a "version N, date, commit" line plus a closing changelog. It needs one rule for what the date means first.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the question. The "3die publishing" decision is less about choosing a design than about **declaring structure the repo already implies**: a tree of papers with typed dependencies, a living page versus frozen editions, and a publisher distinct from a personal site. Each external system the press might want depends on that declaration. ISSN eligibility depends on a publisher series rather than a personal site. Google Scholar needs per-paper metadata and a plain-link index. Zenodo needs version boundaries. An honest interconnection view needs `uses` edges. One registry change in `pages.meta.json`, rendered by `site.py`, can feed all four, which keeps the imprint inside the site track's existing pipeline rather than creating a new system.

The two real risks are quiet ones. The first is identity. An "imprint of the label's authors" with one author risks reading as a personal site, and the ISSN rules say so explicitly, so the plan should either recruit a second editorial name or scope the imprint honestly as iori's series published by 3die. The second is immutability. Shared code between papers means today's "version" of 0.3 is not a fixed object, and no DOI or ISBN means much until a version can be pinned.
