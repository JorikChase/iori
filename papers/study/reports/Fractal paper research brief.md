# Flat noise meets power-law signals: what actually holds

Research brief for iori's paper series, compiled 2026-10-11 from five sets of research notes (image diffusion processes, the spectral argument, language models, landscapes and speedups, Synthetic Silviculture). Every claim below carries a source link; derivations made by the researchers rather than quoted from a paper are labelled as such; everything that could not be verified from a primary source is collected in the flag table at the end.

**BLUF.** The strongest statement that survives contact with the primary sources is narrower than "diffusion is Brownian and generation order is dictated by the power spectrum", but it is still a good one. Stable Diffusion 1.x/2.x/SDXL really do run a discretised Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (Brownian motion with a restoring drift) on a 4-channel VAE latent, while SD3 and FLUX run a deterministic straight-line interpolation between latent and one Gaussian draw; what is true of *every* family, Brownian or not, is that each noise level is additive isotropic Gaussian noise and the optimal denoiser is Tweedie's formula, `E[μ|z] = z + σ² ∇ log f(z)` ([Efron 2011](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)). **No paper in the coarse-to-fine literature states a theorem that generation order is dictated by the spectrum**: Rissanen et al. prove only that expected power spectra add, Dieleman calls the spectral-autoregression picture an in-expectation approximation, Guth et al. prove step-count bounds (κ ∼ L^η), and the only closed-form power-law Wiener filter inside a diffusion paper is a two-month-old preprint ([Hunt, Kamb & Ganguli 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)); the paper has to state and prove the ordering theorem itself, which for Gaussian fields is a short argument. For text, no shipped diffusion LM generates structure first; the one quantitative trajectory study finds **entities committed at normalised step 0.385, punctuation at 0.631** ([Wang, Devasier & Li 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31564)), roughly the reverse of outline-first, while text does carry scale-free memory above the sentence (**Hurst H ≈ 0.69–0.75** on literary corpora, [Montemurro & Pury 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0201139)). The fractal in Sohl-Dickstein 2024 is the trainability boundary in hyperparameter space (box-counting dimension 1.17–1.98 for width-16 MLPs), not the loss landscape, and every known scale-separation speedup (FMM, H-matrices, multigrid, wavelets, FFT, butterfly, Monarch) is conditional on a specific provable regularity. Synthetic Silviculture is reimplementable from the published formulas at module or bud scale; its headline scene is **468,199 plants and 2.27 M modules stepping in 18.8 s on a GTX 1050** ([Makowski et al. 2019](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)), and the only documented open port is a C++20 project with no browser version.

## 1. Image diffusion: additive Gaussian at every level, Brownian only in the older families

### DDPM through SDXL run a discretised Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on a VAE latent

The DDPM forward kernel is `q(x_t | x_{t−1}) = N(x_t; √(1−β_t) x_{t−1}, β_t I)`, which closes to `q(x_t | x_0) = N(x_t; √ᾱ_t x_0, (1−ᾱ_t) I)` with `ᾱ_t = ∏ α_s`, over **T = 1000 steps with β rising linearly from 10⁻⁴ to 0.02** ([Ho, Jain & Abbeel 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)). Song et al. showed that this chain, `x_i = √(1−β_i) x_{i−1} + √β_i z_{i−1}`, converges to the variance-preserving SDE `dx = −½ β(t) x dt + √β(t) dw`, "where w is a standard Wiener process", while the NCSN/SMLD chain converges to the driftless variance-exploding SDE `dx = √(d[σ²(t)]/dt) dw` ([Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)). So the honest description of the DDPM family is **Brownian motion with a linear restoring drift, i.e. a time-inhomogeneous Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process**, variance held near one, discretised as a Markov chain of 1000 Gaussian kernels; the VE family is literally Brownian motion on the clock σ²(t), with no qualifier needed beyond the time change.

Stable Diffusion 1.x and 2.x are "learning the reverse process of a fixed Markov Chain of length T" on `z = E(x)` rather than pixels, with the ε-prediction objective `E[‖ε − ε_θ(z_t, t)‖²]` and t uniform on {1,…,T} ([Rombach et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10752)). Neither the LDM paper nor the SDXL paper prints its β range; the released configs do. SD v1 fixes `timesteps: 1000, linear_start: 0.00085, linear_end: 0.0120` ([CompVis v1-inference.yaml](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/configs/stable-diffusion/v1-inference.yaml)), and the "linear" schedule in that codebase is in fact `betas = linspace(√linear_start, √linear_end, n)²` ([CompVis util.py](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/ldm/modules/diffusionmodules/util.py)), which Diffusers names `scaled_linear` ([SD 1.5 scheduler_config](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)). SDXL keeps the identical range (`beta_start 0.00085, beta_end 0.012`, ε-prediction, 1000 steps) ([SDXL scheduler_config](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)), describes itself as "a discrete-time diffusion schedule" citing Ho et al., and adds **offset noise at level 0.05** in its final stage because it "requires offset-noise for aesthetically pleasing results" ([Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952)). The researchers computed from these numbers that the scaled-linear schedule reaches ᾱ_T ≈ 0.0047 rather than zero, the known non-zero terminal-SNR defect; that figure is a computation from the configs, not a quoted result.

### SD3 and FLUX replace the SDE with a straight line, but every marginal is still Gaussian noise

Rectified flow defines the linear interpolation `X_t = t·X_1 + (1−t)·X_0` between a Gaussian draw and a data point and fits an ODE velocity `v` by least squares; the paper itself notes the interpolation is "non-causal" (needs the endpoint) and that fitting `v` "causalizes" it ([Liu, Gong & Liu 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03003)). Flow matching gives the same construction with Gaussian conditional paths, and shows that VE and VP diffusion paths are special cases while the OT path makes particles "always move in straight line trajectories and with constant speed" ([Lipman et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)). SD3 fixes the forward process as **`z_t = (1−t)·x_0 + t·ε`, applied in latent space**, and, crucially for the paper, rewrites its objective as a weighted noise-prediction loss with weight `w_t = t/(1−t)`, stating that "the optimum of the above objective does not change when introducing a time-dependent weighting" ([Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)). The reference code noises literally as `sigma * noise + (1.0 − sigma) * latent_image` ([Stability-AI sd3_impls.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_impls.py)). FLUX.1 is "a 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer" ([FLUX.1-dev model card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev)); its official sampler draws `torch.randn` once and steps `img = img + (t_prev − t_curr) * pred` ([black-forest-labs sampling.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/sampling.py)). Black Forest Labs promised a technical report in August 2024 ([bfl.ai blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/24-08-01-bfl)) that the researchers could not find; FLUX.2 (November 2025) is known only through secondary sources ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/12/01/open-source-image-generator-startup-black-forest-labs-raises-300m/), [Prodia docs](https://docs.prodia.com/models/flux-2/)).

The consequence is a two-level statement. For a fixed pair `(x_0, ε)` the SD3/FLUX path is a straight line with no Brownian increments, so "Brownian" is false of the path. But `z_t | x_0 ~ N((1−t) x_0, t² I)`, so every level is additive isotropic Gaussian noise with EDM-equivalent σ = t/(1−t). The researchers derived (their own algebra, not a quoted result) that an Itô SDE with the same marginals exists, `dx = −x/(1−t) dt + √(2t/(1−t)) dw`, consistent with SD3's own statement that rectified flow is a diffusion objective under a reweighting and with Liu et al.'s remark that all PF-ODEs are instances of `α_t X_1 + β_t ξ`. The safe wording for the paper: the *marginals* coincide with those of an OU-type Brownian diffusion; the *path used in training and sampling* is deterministic.

EDM parametrises everything by σ directly: "the family of mollified distributions p(x; σ) obtained by adding i.i.d. Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ", with the probability-flow ODE `dx = −σ̇ σ ∇ log p(x; σ) dt` and the recommendation σ(t) = t, s(t) = 1 ([Karras et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)). Its stochastic generalisation `dx± = −σ̇σ ∇log p dt ± β(t)σ² ∇log p dt + √(2β) σ dω_t` recovers Song's SDEs at β = σ̇/σ and the ODE at β = 0, which is the cleanest single formula relating the families; training corrupts with one Gaussian draw `n = randn_like(y) * sigma` and never simulates a forward SDE ([NVlabs/edm loss.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/training/loss.py)). Consistency models are built on the EDM PF-ODE with **T = 80, ε = 0.002**, and distillation leaves the forward process untouched: the noising is still `x + t·z` ([Song, Dhariwal, Chen & Sutskever 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)). All distilled families (consistency, LCM, guidance-distilled FLUX.1 dev/schnell) inherit their teacher's forward process and only change the reverse map.

One point the paper should not gloss: SD-family noise is Gaussian in a *learned, compressed* coordinate system and only approximately so in pixel coordinates through a nonlinear decoder, which matters for any comparison to a physical Brownian field in the image plane.

### The reverse process is Anderson's SDE in theory and a deterministic ODE in practice

DDPM's Algorithm 2 is stochastic ancestral sampling, `x_{t−1} = (1/√α_t)(x_t − ((1−α_t)/√(1−ᾱ_t)) ε_θ) + σ_t z`, and Song et al. showed it "actually corresponds to one special discretization of the reverse-time VP SDE" `dx = [f − g² ∇ log p_t] dt + g dw̄`, attributing the time reversal to Anderson: "A remarkable result from Anderson 1982 states that the reverse of a diffusion process is also a diffusion process" ([Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)). The probability-flow ODE `dx = [f − ½ g² ∇ log p_t] dt` shares the SDE's marginals, and DDIM at η = 0 is an Euler method on that ODE ([Song, Meng & Ermon 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02502)). In practice, every 2024–2026 production sampler is deterministic: SD 1.5 ships `PNDMScheduler`, SDXL ships `EulerDiscreteScheduler` and used "50 steps of the DDIM sampler" in its figures ([Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952)), EDM uses Heun's second-order method with optional `S_churn` re-noising ([NVlabs/edm generate.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/generate.py)), SD3 says "we always use a Euler discretization" and its reference defaults to `dpmpp_2m` at 40 steps ([sd3_infer.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_infer.py)), FLUX steps Euler, and Diffusers' flow scheduler exposes `stochastic_sampling` off by default ([diffusers scheduler](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py)). The Brownian reverse SDE is the theoretical object; the only randomness actually drawn in a typical SD3/FLUX run is the initial Gaussian.

### Tweedie, Anderson, Vincent: the three identities the paper leans on

Efron states Tweedie's formula for `μ ~ g`, `z | μ ~ N(μ, σ²)` as, verbatim, "E{μ|z} = z + σ² l′(z) where l′(z) = d/dz log f(z)" (his Eq. 1.4), credits it via Robbins (1956) to personal correspondence with Maurice Kenneth Tweedie, and adds the posterior variance `σ²(1 + σ² l″(z))` (Eq. 2.8), with the reading "unbiased estimate plus Bayes correction" ([Efron 2011](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)). EDM writes the same identity in diffusion notation: minimise `E‖D(y + n; σ) − y‖²`, "then ∇_x log p(x; σ) = (D(x; σ) − x)/σ²", and its Figure 1(b) shows the ideal denoiser approaching the dataset mean as σ grows ([Karras et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)). Because every family above corrupts with additive isotropic Gaussian noise, "the network is a denoiser" and "the network estimates the score" are the same statement exactly, and the paper can say so without qualification. The researchers' translations into VP units, `E[x_0 | x_t] = (x_t + (1−ᾱ_t) ∇ log p_t)/√ᾱ_t`, and rectified-flow units, `E[x_0 | z_t] = (z_t + t² ∇ log p_t)/(1−t)`, are derivations from Efron's Eq. 1.4 and the forward definitions; no fetched source prints all three.

Anderson's paper is "Reverse-time diffusion equation models", *Stochastic Processes and their Applications* 12(3), 313–326 (1982), DOI 10.1016/0304-4149(82)90051-5 ([ANU record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/reverse-time-diffusion-equation-models/)); the ScienceDirect page returned 403, so the formula is cited through Song et al. and Karras et al., both of whom name it as the source. Vincent's result that the denoising-autoencoder criterion "amounts to matching the data score of an energy-based model with that of a nonparametric Parzen density estimate" is *Neural Computation* 23(7):1661–1674 (2011), read from a secondary host ([gwern.net copy](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/diffusion/2011-vincent.pdf)); Song et al.'s continuous objective is built "on denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011)". Miyasawa's "An empirical Bayes estimator of the mean of a normal population", *Bull. Inter. Statist. Inst.* 38, 181–187, could only be found as a bibliographic entry with a 1960/1961 date discrepancy ([T&F reference list](https://www.informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/03610927508827311)).

**What this means for the paper.** The paper can say, with citations, that the noise in Stable Diffusion 1.x–SDXL is a 1000-step Gaussian Markov chain that is the Euler–Maruyama discretisation of the VP-SDE `dx = −½β x dt + √β dw`, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process driven by a Wiener process, on a 4-channel latent, with Anderson's reversal solved as a deterministic ODE in practice. For SD3 and FLUX it must say instead that the forward process is a straight-line interpolation whose marginals match an OU-type diffusion but whose path samples no Wiener increments. The sentence that stays true for every family is "additive isotropic Gaussian noise at every level, with the optimal denoiser given by Tweedie's formula", and that sentence, not "Brownian motion", is what the spectral argument in Section 2 actually needs.

## 2. The spectral argument: a derivation everyone repeats and nobody proves

### No theorem says generation order follows the power spectrum

The researchers read the primary texts of Dieleman 2024, Rissanen et al. 2023 (including Appendix A.5), Hoogeboom & Salimans 2023, Guth et al. 2022 (Theorems 1–3), both Kadkhodaie et al. papers, Falck et al. 2025 and Hunt et al. 2026, and found **no theorem environment anywhere stating that the order in which a diffusion model generates is dictated by the data power spectrum**. Dieleman's claim is that "diffusion models of images perform approximate autoregression in the frequency domain!", scoped "at least for visual data", and explicitly "in expectation, averaged across many images", with an SNR threshold he calls "quite arbitrary" and a filtering equivalence that holds "only because of the characteristic shape of natural image spectra" ([Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)). His evidence is a radially averaged power spectrum of Imagenette (green channel, log-domain average) with a fitted exponent **α̂ = 2.454**, the flat spectrum of Gaussian noise, noisy-image spectra whose hinge moves to lower frequencies as variance rises, and a maximal detectable frequency f_max(t) for rectified flow defined by the SNR-1 condition `R[x₀](f) > (t/(1−t))²`. The same post supplies its own counterexample: GTZAN audio waveforms have a spectrum that is neither a power law nor monotonic, so "the 'diffusion is just spectral autoregression' meme does not apply to audio waveforms". He credits Rissanen et al. §2.2 as the direct inspiration.

Rissanen et al. write that "the PSD of natural images obeys an approximate power law 1/f^α, where often α ≈ 2", that isotropic noise and image PSDs "are additive in expectation", and "thus, in the reverse process, the diffusion model generates frequencies starting from the coarse-grained structure and progressing toward fine details" ([Rissanen, Heinonen & Solin 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397)). The only formal content is Appendix A.5, equations 52–57: with noise covariance γ²I, **E_ε[PSD(u + ε)_i] = PSD(u)_i + γ²**, which they call "a formal explanation for the idea that isotropic noise effectively drowns out the frequency components in the data with a lower PSD than the variance of the noise". The step from erasure order to generation order is asserted, and the paper notes the bias was "concurrently" observed in Kreis, Gao & Vahdat's CVPR 2022 tutorial, which the researchers did not retrieve. Hoogeboom & Salimans prove an exact bookkeeping identity rather than an ordering theorem: with `u_t = Vᵀ z_t` in the DCT basis, `q(u_t | u_x) = N(d_t·u_x, σ² I)` with `d_t = exp(−λ τ_t)` is "a special case of the standard Gaussian diffusion process", Markov transitions have `σ²_{t|s} = (1 − (d_t/d_s)²) σ²`, so blurring "add[s] more noise on the higher frequencies per timestep"; their inductive-bias remark is qualitative ("retains low frequencies over higher frequencies") and never mentions 1/f spectra ([Hoogeboom & Salimans 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05557)).

The Mallat-school papers prove things about *cost*, not order. Guth et al.'s Theorem 1, for Gaussian data with covariance condition number κ, bounds the step count by **T/δ ≤ C ε⁻² κ³**, and the commentary says that for stationary image processes "the covariance eigenvalues are given by the power spectrum, which typically decays like |ω|⁻¹", so "κ is proportional to a power of the image size", numerically κ ∼ L^η; Theorem 3 then shows that renormalised wavelet coefficients of a power-law process P(ω) ∼ (ξ^η + |ω|^η)⁻¹ can be sampled with **T/δ ≤ C ε⁻², independent of dimension**, which is why their generator goes coarse to fine ([Guth, Coste, De Bortoli & Mallat 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05003)). Coarse-to-fine is a design choice justified by conditioning, and the theorem is about *their* algorithm's step count. Their "η = 1" and "|ω|⁻¹" read as 1/f rather than 1/f², and the researchers could not settle from the PDF whether |ω| indexes eigenvalues or radial frequency; flag before quoting as a 1/f² citation. Kadkhodaie, Guth, Mallat & Simoncelli 2023 *assume* a coarse-to-fine factorisation ("conditional probabilities of its wavelet coefficients conditioned by coarser scale coefficients … local and stationary") and show conditional receptive fields as small as 9×9 suffice ([Kadkhodaie et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02984)). Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli & Mallat 2024 (ICLR Outstanding Paper) contains the Wiener gain without the word Wiener: the oracle shrinkage in a fixed basis is minimised at **λ_k(x) = ⟨x,e_k⟩²/(⟨x,e_k⟩² + σ²)**, "a soft threshold" that is ≈1 when signal dominates and ≈0 when noise does, giving oracle error ∼ Σ_k min(⟨x,e_k⟩², σ²) and, for coefficients decaying as k^{−(α+1)}, **MSE ∼ σ^{2α/(α+1)}** ([Kadkhodaie et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557)); the paper's substance is the geometry-adaptive harmonic bases found in trained denoisers' Jacobians, and it never mentions Fourier power laws or low-frequency-first generation.

Two recent papers come closest. Falck et al. state the mechanism in SNR terms, "the SNR of low frequencies is orders of magnitudes higher than the SNR of high frequencies at all timesteps under a DDPM forward process … a DDPM forward process does not 'noise all frequencies equally'", show the power law on CIFAR-10 images, video, audio and cryo-EM maps, and build an **"EqualSNR" forward process that removes the hierarchy and "performs on par with DDPM"** on image benchmarks; their Propositions 1–3 concern SNR, covariance and the Gaussianity of the reverse kernel, not ordering ([Falck et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11278)). Hunt, Kamb & Ganguli write, for a power-law Gaussian field P(k) = A|k|^{−α}, the closed-form Fourier Wiener filter **ŵ(σ², k) = 1/(1 + A⁻¹ σ² |k|^α)** (their eq. 198) and the renormalisation condition ŵ(βσ², k) = ŵ(σ², β^{1/α} k), so "the filter used for a given noise level σ² is simply a rescaled and dilated version of the filter at any other given noise level", with a spectral scale L_spec = (σ_t²/A)^{1/α} "where the noise power σ_t² equals the signal power"; their theorem is about a critical patch scale, not order, and they cite Kamb & Ganguli and Lukoianov et al. for the observation that "image generation proceeds starting from a large coarse spatial scale to a small fine one" ([Hunt et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)). This is a July 2026 preprint, unreviewed.

### Natural images have β ≈ 2, with a spread of about half a unit

The classic measurement is a central tendency, not a constant. Simoncelli & Olshausen's review states that "many authors have found that the spectral power of natural images falls with frequency, f, according to a power law, 1/f^p, with estimated values for p typically near 2", and lists three explanations: scale invariance ("only a Fourier spectrum that falls as a power law will retain its shape"), edges ("edges themselves have a 1/f² power spectrum"), and object size distributions ([Simoncelli & Olshausen 2001](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/eero/simoncelli01.pdf)). Torralba & Oliva attribute the observation to "Burton and Moorhead 1987, Field 1987, 1994, Tolhurst et al 1992", fit A/f^α per image on about 12,000 scenes at 256×256 over 0.02–0.35 cycles/pixel, and tabulate **natural scenes α = 1.98 (SD 0.58) horizontal, 2.02 (0.53) oblique, 2.22 (0.55) vertical; man-made 1.83 (0.58), 2.37 (0.45), 2.07 (0.52)** ([Torralba & Oliva 2003](https://web.mit.edu/torralba/www/ne3302.pdf)). Ruderman & Bialek, on woodland scenes, fit S(k) = A/k^{2−η} over about 2.5 decades with **η = 0.19 ± 0.01**, i.e. β ≈ 1.81, and show local contrast distributions across box sizes that "all lie atop one another" ([Ruderman & Bialek 1993, NIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1993/file/4d5b995358e7798bc7e9d9db83c612a5-Paper.pdf); journal version [PRL 73, 814 (1994)](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.814)); Ruderman's 1994 review opens with "perhaps the most notable property of natural images is an invariance to scale" ([Network 5:517, abstract only](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0954-898X_5_4_006)). The mechanism for 2 − η is an object-size distribution p(s) ∝ s^{−(3+η)} ([Zylberberg, Pfau & DeWeese 2012](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/zylberberg2012dead.pdf)). Set against Dieleman's 2.454 on Imagenette and Hunt et al.'s "α ≈ 2 − ε, ε typically 0.1–0.3", the exponent is **dataset-dependent across roughly 1.8–2.5 with per-image SD ≈ 0.5**, so any argument needing an exact exponent (an exact Hurst value, say) has to be an ensemble statement.

Field 1987 (*J. Opt. Soc. Am. A* 4(12):2379, DOI 10.1364/JOSAA.4.002379) could not be opened: the Optica interstitial and PubMed cookie wall both blocked the fetch, two PDF mirrors were dead, so its wording and image count are unverified and the β ≈ 2 attribution rests on the three reviews quoting it. "Daniel & Tauber" in the brief could not be identified as any paper on natural-image statistics and should be treated as non-existent (possibly a conflation with Daniel Ruderman) until a citation is supplied.

### One schedule across scales: justified in scale-relative coordinates, refuted in raw pixel SNR

No paper says in so many words that a single noise schedule works across scales because images are scale-invariant. The two theorem-level results that come closest are Guth et al.'s Theorem 3 (same step count at every wavelet scale for a power-law Gaussian process, because renormalised wavelet coefficients have dimension-independent conditioning via Meyer 1992) and Hunt et al.'s scale-equivariance of the Wiener family. The pixel-domain literature says the opposite for raw resolution changes: simple diffusion observes that average-pooling a 2×2 block of z_t leaves "only … half the amount of noise", hypothesises that at high resolution "much less diffusion time is spent on the lower resolution, a stage at which the global consistency is generated", and shifts the schedule with resolution ([Hoogeboom, Heek & Salimans 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11093)); Chen finds "the optimal noise scheduling … shifts towards a noisier one (due to increased redundancy in pixels)" and recommends scaling the input by b, "equivalent to shifting the logSNR by log b" ([Chen 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10972)). SD3's α = 3 shift and FLUX's token-count-dependent μ (Section 1) are the production descendants of that observation. The synthesis, which the researchers note nobody in the set states, is that on power-law data noise level and spatial scale are one coordinate (σ² ↔ |k|^α; cutoff f ∝ σ^{−2/α}), so one schedule in the scale-relative variable is justified by the spectrum while one schedule in raw pixel SNR is not. Falck's EqualSNR result cuts the other way and deserves a sentence: the coarse-to-fine hierarchy is a *consequence* of white noise meeting a power-law spectrum, not a *requirement* for good samples.

### Fractional Brownian spectra, and which object the paper is identifying

For fBm in one dimension the spectral exponent 2H+1 goes back to Mandelbrot & Van Ness's proportionality argument and was made rigorous as a time-averaged Wigner–Ville spectrum by Flandrin, "On the spectrum of fractional Brownian motions", *IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory* 35(1):197–199 (1989), with the increments (fGn) at exponent 2H−1 ([review arXiv:2410.14205](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14205); [Flandrin lecture notes](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/patrick.flandrin/lns95.pdf)). In d dimensions β = d + 2H, hence 2H+2 for surfaces ([arXiv:0711.2892](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.2892); [arXiv:1107.5287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.5287)), with Voss's profile relation β = 2H+1 and D = E + (3−β)/2 and a warning that 2-D Fourier β and profile β differ ([Symmetry 2016](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/8/7/66/html)); Kube & Pentland show a Lambertian fractal surface with spectrum ∝ f^β images with spectrum ∝ f^{2−β} ([MIT Media Lab record](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/publications/on-the-imaging-of-fractal-surfaces-2)). Matching 1/f² image statistics to a 2-D fBm surface therefore gives H = 0 (the edge of the fBm range) and to a 1-D profile gives H = ½ (ordinary Brownian); the paper must say which object (profile vs surface, intensity vs height) it identifies, since shading changes the exponent. Mandelbrot & Van Ness 1968 and Voss 1988 were not opened; the 2H+2 attribution is secondary.

**What this means for the paper.** The literature supports the four-step chain as a theorem for Gaussian or fBm fields and as an empirical regularity for photographs: white noise is flat (Rissanen A.5), the signal is a power law (Flandrin for fBm; β ≈ 2 with spread ≈ 0.5 and dataset dependence 1.81–2.45 for photographs), the posterior-mean denoiser is the Wiener gain S/(S+σ²) that erases modes in order of decreasing frequency (Kadkhodaie 2024 eq. 11–12, Hunt et al. eq. 198–199), and Anderson time reversal of that erasure yields coarse-to-fine generation. Step 4 is asserted rather than proved by Rissanen, Dieleman and Falck alike, so the paper's contribution is to write steps 3–4 out as the theorem the diffusion literature keeps gesturing at, citing Rissanen/Kreis for the observation, Dieleman for the phrase, Falck for the SNR framing and the EqualSNR counterfactual, Guth for the conditioning cost κ ∼ L^η, and Hunt et al. for the closed-form filter.

## 3. Language models: parallel and tunable, but not structure-first

### What diffusion LMs diffuse, and how they pick an order

Every shipped diffusion language model diffuses discrete tokens through an absorbing [MASK] state, not Gaussian noise on embeddings. D3PM introduced structured transition matrices including an absorbing-state matrix that "links diffusion to autoregressive and mask-based models" ([Austin et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03006)); SEDD's score-entropy loss cut perplexity 25–75% against earlier diffusion LMs and matched GPT-2 at comparable size with "32× fewer network evaluations" for similar quality ([Lou, Meng & Ermon, ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834)); MDLM's Rao-Blackwellised objective is "a mixture of classical masked language modeling losses" ([Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524)). LLaDA, the first 8B-scale instance, is "a forward data masking process and a reverse generation process, parameterized by a Transformer to predict masked tokens", trained on 2.3 T tokens for 0.13 M H800 GPU-hours ([Nie et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992)). Dream 7B is initialised from Qwen2.5 weights and trained on 580 B tokens ([HKU NLP blog](https://hkunlp.github.io/blog/2025/dream/); [Ye et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15487)); BD3-LM interpolates between diffusion and AR by sampling "one block at a time, conditioned on previously sampled blocks" with block sizes 4, 8, 16, never remasking within a block and keeping a KV cache across blocks ([Arriola et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09573v3)). Mercury describes "a diffusion process over token sequences" that "starts from random noise and iteratively refines the output … in a coarse-to-fine way" and discloses neither noise type, step count nor ordering ([Inception Labs 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17298)); Gemini Diffusion's page says it "generates entire blocks of tokens at once" by "refining noise, step-by-step" and "corrects errors during generation", with no description of order and status "experimental demo" ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-diffusion/)).

The resolution order is set by a confidence heuristic, not by any linguistic hierarchy. LLaDA's default is **low-confidence remasking**: at each step the least-confident predictions are remasked and the rest kept; semi-autoregressive block sampling, added without retraining, lifted the Instruct model's GSM8K from 69.4 to 78.6 and MATH from 31.9 to 42.2, because EOS padding in SFT data otherwise causes early termination ([Nie et al. 2025, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09992)). Samplers generally "select a fixed (or adaptive) number of positions with the highest posterior probability and convert them from [MASK] to concrete tokens", so flat-posterior positions are resolved late and an oracle order "prioritizes easier positions first" ([Re-evaluating Confidence Remasking 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12232v1); [Confidence-Based Decoding is Provably Efficient 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22248)). The researchers' reading is that "coarse-to-fine" in Mercury's and Google's copy is image-diffusion vocabulary with no mechanism behind it: in masked discrete diffusion there is no frequency axis, a token is masked or it is not.

### The measured trajectories run roughly backwards from outline-first

Two studies quantify order. DiffuCoder defines local and global **AR-ness@k** and finds dLLMs "do not always decode in a purely AR manner" but both metrics are "closer to 1 than 0"; models adapted from AR weights (Dream, DiffuCoder) are more left-to-right than LLaDA trained from scratch; higher temperature "substantially lowers AR-ness"; first-step confidence shows an "L"-shaped "entropy sink" immediately right of the prefix; and on code the model "tends to produce later tokens first, leaving some early masked tokens un-recovered until much later" ([Gong et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20639v2)). The graph-to-text study logs the normalised step at which each token is committed in LLaDA-8B on WebNLG: **entity 0.385, stop word 0.576, relation 0.597, punctuation 0.631, other 0.633**, so "MDLMs naturally prioritize entities first, followed by relational and function words, with structural tokens resolved last"; Dream-7B shows the same entity-first order (0.423). After SFT, LLaDA "prematurely anchor[s]" sentence ends: the mean first-period step drops from 0.720 to 0.385 and content committed before the first period falls from 60.9% to 17.8%; a training-free confidence multiplier λ = 0.25 on structural tokens restores 89.9% and +9.4 BLEU-4, validated on single runs and LLaDA only ([Wang, Devasier & Li 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31564)). A June 2026 paper argues directly that token-wise diffusion LMs keep "training corruption and inference commitment at the token level throughout denoising", which at high noise "leaves scattered local fragments rather than coherent evidence, which makes early coarse structure hard to form", and proposes committing coherent token groups at high noise to get "earlier skeleton formation" ([Li, Shi & Yan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21802)). Apple's PLANNER paragraph-latent model shows a review going from vague to detailed over 10 steps, an illustration rather than a measurement ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/latent-language-diffusion-model)). No paper measures commit time against syntactic depth or discourse position on long open-ended generations.

### Multi-token prediction amortises left-to-right; it does not reorder anything

DeepSeek-V3's MTP "uses D sequential modules to predict D additional tokens" and "keep[s] the complete causal chain at each prediction depth", with embedding and output head shared with the main model, **D = 1**, loss weight **λ = 0.3 for the first 10 T tokens then 0.1**; at inference the modules can be discarded or "repurpose[d] … for speculative decoding", where "the acceptance rate of the second token prediction ranges between 85% and 90%" and delivers "1.8 times TPS", with no hardware or batch size stated ([DeepSeek-AI 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437)). The ablation shows MTP lifting a small MoE's HumanEval from 20.7 to 26.8 and GSM8K from 25.4 to 31.4, and a large MoE's HumanEval from 44.5 to 53.7, with MMLU the one decline (67.5 → 66.6). The 85–90% figure in the brief is therefore correct. Gloeckle et al. (Meta FAIR) train n independent heads on a shared trunk; 13B models solve "12% more problems on HumanEval and 17% more on MBPP" and are "up to 3 times faster at inference" ([Gloeckle et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)). Medusa adds heads with tree-attention verification for 2.2× (frozen backbone) to 2.3–3.6× (joint fine-tuning) ([Cai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774)). Speculative decoding itself is from Leviathan, Kalman & Matias (outputs "identical to the standard implementation", 2–3× on T5-XXL, [ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)) and Chen et al. (modified rejection sampling that "preserves the distribution of the target model", 2–2.5× on Chinchilla 70B, [DeepMind 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318)).

Who ships it: a March 2026 paper states the DeepSeek-style design "has been widely integrated into prominent industrial LLMs, such as MiMo, GLM, LongCat, and Qwen3-Next" ([arXiv:2603.17942](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17942v1)); vLLM serves Qwen3-Next with `method: qwen3_next_mtp, num_speculative_tokens: 2` and NVIDIA publishes an MTP training recipe ([Megatron-Bridge docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/megatron-bridge/0.6.0/training/multi-token-prediction.html)); NVIDIA's TensorRT-LLM implements DeepSeek-R1 MTP as the draft stage ([NVIDIA blog](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/latest/blogs/tech_blog/blog02_DeepSeek_R1_MTP_Implementation_and_Optimization.html)). Apple finds existing AR models hold "a surprising amount of internal knowledge about upcoming tokens" and reports ~5× on code/math and ~2.5× on chat after gated-LoRA fine-tuning ([Samragh et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11851v1)). No evidence was found that Meta ships MTP in any Llama, and whether DeepSeek's own serving enables MTP drafting is unconfirmed from a primary source. The researchers' verdict, which the paper should adopt: MTP is a training-time auxiliary objective that doubles as a draft model; speculative decoding leaves the base model's distribution unchanged; **none of these methods changes the order in which text is committed**, so they are irrelevant as evidence for or against structure-first generation.

### Text has memory at every scale above the sentence, and Markov generators cannot fake it

The established laws stack by level rather than forming one spectrum. Zipf holds robustly with α ≈ 1 and Zipf–Mandelbrot shift β ≈ 2.7 across the ANC, Google Books and 17-language Swadesh lists, with its cause "unresolved" ([Piantadosi 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4176592/)); Heaps' exponent ties to Zipf's by β = 1/α only in the infinite-system limit ([Lü, Zhang & Zhou 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2996287/); [Font-Clos & Corral](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.0705)). Word recurrence is bursty: inter-occurrence gaps fit a stretched exponential depending mainly on semantic type ([Altmann, Pierrehumbert & Motter 2009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2770836/)), and on ten long single-author texts in five languages rare-word return intervals follow a Weibull with **β ≈ 0.7** and are long-range correlated with autocorrelation exponent γ = 0.14–0.48, shuffling restoring β ≈ 1 ([Tanaka-Ishii & Bunde 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0164658)). Rescaled-range analysis of rank series gives **Hurst H = 0.687 ± 0.040 (Shakespeare, 885k words), 0.738 ± 0.033 (Dickens, 5.6 M), 0.745 ± 0.045 (Darwin), 0.690 (Webster's 1913)**, against sentence-shuffled ≈ 0.57, rank-shuffled ≈ 0.52 and 7-letter Markov text 0.533, so syntax alone is not enough and the memory lives in corpus-wide word association ([Montemurro & Pury 2002](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0201139)). Google DeepMind's LLM-based estimate agrees: language is "self-similar, exhibiting complexities at all levels of granularity" with Hurst ≈ 0.7, robust across domains and architectures ([Alabdulmohsin, Tran & Dehghani 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01825)). Sentence-length spectra "universally develop a convincing '1/f^β' scaling with the average exponent β ≈ 1/2" over about two decades, flat after shuffling, with the correlations carried by full-stop recurrence rather than frequent words ([Drożdż et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.8319)); letter-pair mutual information decays as a power law over "a few hundred letters" in Moby Dick ([Ebeling & Pöschel 1994](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9309005)); correlations "flow from highly structured linguistic levels down to the building blocks of a text" ([Altmann, Cristadoro & Degli Esposti 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3406867)).

The theoretical bridge to hierarchy is Lin & Tegmark: mutual information "decays exponentially in any probabilistic regular grammar" but can decay as a power law in context-free (recursive) grammars; measured on Wikipedia, French, English, Bach and human chromosome 5, "all measured curves are seen to decay roughly as power laws", a bigram captures "less than a fifth" of the MI an LSTM does, and LSTMs still "under-predict long-range mutual information" ([Lin & Tegmark 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.06737)). The caveat the paper must carry: in Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii's five-property evaluation, gated RNNs "are the only computational models that can reproduce the long memory behavior", and **a PCFG does not**, which cuts against a naive "hierarchy ⇒ power law" story and points to topical/semantic memory as the real driver ([Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii 2019](https://aclanthology.org/J19-3003/)). No paper measures Hurst, Taylor or MI-decay exponents on text generated by a diffusion LM, and none connects outline-first planning to these exponents.

**What this means for the paper.** A paper on diffusion over the whole document can say that masked-diffusion LMs resolve a document in parallel in a tunable, non-left-to-right order, and that text demonstrably carries scale-free memory above the sentence (H ≈ 0.7, 1/f^{1/2} sentence-length spectra, power-law MI decay) that Markov, n-gram and even PCFG generators do not reproduce. It cannot say that any existing diffusion LM generates structure first: the only measurements show confidence-ordered unmasking commits entities first and structural tokens last, SFT models anchor sentence ends prematurely, and the vendors' "coarse-to-fine" is unmeasured. The experiment that would make the claim is cheap to specify: log per-token commit step against syntactic depth or discourse position on long generations, and measure Hurst/Taylor/MI exponents of diffusion versus AR output at equal size.

## 4. Landscapes and speedups: a fractal boundary, and gains that are always conditional

### Sohl-Dickstein's fractal is the trainability boundary, not the loss surface

The object that is fractal in Sohl-Dickstein 2024 is the boundary, in a 2-D plane of hyperparameters, between training runs that converge and runs that diverge, "fractal over more than ten decades of scale in all tested configurations", by analogy with Mandelbrot and Julia sets ([Sohl-Dickstein 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06184)). The networks are tiny: one hidden layer, input and hidden width **n = 16**, scalar MSE output, mean-field parameterisation, **272 examples** (equal to the parameter count) for nonlinear nets, full-batch steepest descent for 500 steps in float64, with the plane spanned by the two per-layer learning rates in five of six conditions and by init mean versus learning rate in the sixth. Box-counting over about 50 successive 2× zooms of 4096² images gives median dimensions of **1.17 (deep linear), 1.20 (ReLU), 1.41 (tanh, single datapoint), 1.55 (tanh, minibatch 16), 1.66 (tanh, full batch), 1.98 (init-mean grid)**, with no error bars; the author flags that zoom regions were chosen by hand, no Lyapunov exponents were computed, the minibatch case is only "suggestive of" Lyapunov fractals, and that good hyperparameters tend to sit near the edge of instability ([full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06184)). The paper is three pages and its peer-review status was not determined. For iori's purposes the key reading is that this is a property of the iterated training map's stability boundary in *hyperparameter* space; it says nothing about the loss surface over weights being fractal, and cannot be borrowed for that purpose.

### SGD as Langevin dynamics is an approximation with four stated assumptions

Mandt, Hoffman & Blei state that "constant SGD simulates a Markov chain with a stationary distribution" ([JMLR 2017](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/17-214.html)) and, to get there, "make four assumptions": Gaussian gradient noise by the CLT with covariance C/S, a constant covariance C = BBᵀ "justified when the iterates of SGD are confined to a small enough region around a local optimum", replacement of the difference equation by the SDE dθ = −εg dt + (ε/√S) B dW when "the gradients or the learning rates are small enough", and a quadratic loss with A the Hessian; the result is an OU process whose stationary covariance "is proportional to the learning rate ε and inversely proportional to the magnitude of A and minibatch size S", and the authors say that in the early search phase "assumptions 1–4 are often violated and it is hard to say anything general" ([JMLR PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume18/17-214/17-214.pdf)). Smith & Le use the same SDE picture to define a noise scale g ≈ εN/B and an optimal batch size B_opt ∝ εN ([ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06451)); Jastrzębski et al. find "higher values of the ratio [learning rate / batch size] lead to wider minima" ([arXiv 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04623)); Smith et al. show increasing batch size reproduces learning-rate decay, reaching 76.1% ImageNet top-1 with ResNet-50 in under 30 minutes ([ICLR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00489)); Welling & Teh's SGLD adds noise of variance ε_t and converges to posterior samples "as we anneal the stepsize" ([ICML 2011](https://icml.cc/imls/conferences/2011/papers/398_icmlpaper.pdf)). No source states "SGD is Langevin dynamics" as a theorem for non-quadratic losses, and Gong et al.'s heavy-tailed step distributions (below) violate the Gaussian-noise assumption outright early in training. "Temperature ∝ lr/batch" is a physics gloss on a linearised covariance, and "learning-rate decay as annealing" is an analogy the SDE picture supports, not a convergence guarantee.

### Hessian spectra, edge of stability, and the two measured fractal dimensions of SGD paths

The Hessian "is composed of two parts: (1) the bulk centered near zero, (2) and outliers away from the bulk"; adding parameters "merely scales the bulk" while changing the data "only affects the outliers" ([Sagun et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04454)). Papyan attributes the outliers to class structure: one top outlier, C − 1 secondary outliers, a "mini-bulk" of C(C − 1) and a main bulk, with the C outliers from the between-class gradient covariance, verified on VGG11 on MNIST and CIFAR-10 ([Papyan, JMLR 2020](https://jmlr2020.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume21/20-933/20-933.pdf)); Ghorbani et al. see "the rapid appearance of large isolated eigenvalues" that batch normalisation almost removes ([Ghorbani, Krishnan & Xiao 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10159)). For full-batch gradient descent "the maximum eigenvalue of the training loss Hessian hovers just above the numerical value 2 / (step size)" ([Cohen et al., ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00065)); for minibatch SGD the saturating quantity is "batch sharpness" while full-batch λ_max stays below 2/η ([Andreyev & Beneventano 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20553)). "Rugged" in this literature means a few sharp class-structured directions over a near-flat bulk, not scale-invariant roughness.

Two papers do measure fractal quantities, both of the trajectory rather than the surface. Gong et al. find SGD "initially … exhibits anomalous superdiffusion, which attenuates gradually and changes to subdiffusion at long times" (ResNet-14, CIFAR-10, batch 1024, lr 0.1; late MSD exponent 0.78), a path fractal dimension **D_f = 2/λ ∈ [1.32, 2.67]** with two scaling regimes, a path that is "self-affine rather than self-similar", α-stable step distributions with stability parameter ≈ 1.47 early and ≈ 1.9 late, and a loss-increment power law with exponent 1.8 over Δr ∈ [0.4, 10] early in training that flattens later ([Gong et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10588)). Tan et al. model SGD as Euler–Maruyama on fractional Brownian motion with per-coordinate Hurst exponents, use Xiao's Hausdorff-dimension formula as a bound, and measure **dim_H ≈ 3.12 ± 0.07 falling to 2.70 ± 0.06** for ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 subsets from 1000 to 4000 examples per class ([Tan et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.04359)). A 2025 preprint argues "SGD is effectively diffusion on a fractal landscape" without numbers in the abstract ([arXiv:2503.22478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22478)). No paper estimates a box-counting or correlation dimension of a realistic loss surface, and none reports a direct Hurst exponent of parameter trajectories; the evidence that "the landscape is fractal" is Gong's one-decade loss-increment power law and should be presented as suggestive. Simulated annealing's source is Kirkpatrick, Gelatt & Vecchi, *Science* 220:671–680 (1983), DOI 10.1126/science.220.4598.671, verified only via Wikipedia because Science.org returned 403 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulated_annealing)).

The common pattern across the table, a hierarchical decomposition with a bounded amount of information crossing each scale, is precisely what a generic dense matrix or a generic non-convex loss surface lacks, so the speedups do not transfer by analogy. Monarch and FlashAttention are the closest to "fractal speedups inside networks" and neither says anything about the loss landscape.

### Fractal image compression: where self-similarity was fitted, and why wavelets absorbed it

Jacquin's 1992 coder exploits "self-transformability on a blockwise basis", the fractal code being a contractive transformation whose iterates converge to the image, aimed at "rates in the range of 0.5–1.0 b/pixel" on monochrome images ([Konstanz abstract](https://uni-konstanz.de/mmsp/fractal2/html/Publi/Jacq92.html)). Secondary sources report JPEG-like results up to about 50:1 and over 170:1 on satellite imagery, but "encoding is extremely computationally expensive because of the search used to find the self-similarities", exhaustive domain search scaling as O(n²) for an n×n image ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_compression); [US 5,850,262](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5850262)); the last commercial holder abandoned it in January 2016. Davis showed that Jacquin-style block coders "are Haar wavelet subtree quantization schemes" and "derive much of their effectiveness from their ability to efficiently represent wavelet zero trees" ([Davis 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18267389)), so smooth-wavelet coders subsume the idea with far cheaper encoding. What survives is the collage theorem: for an IFS with contractivity s < 1, a collage error h(L, ∪ w_n(L)) ≤ ε bounds the attractor error by **ε/(1 − s)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collage_theorem); [arXiv:2505.09560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09560)), the clean statement that self-similarity can be *fitted* with controlled error amplification. No direct fractal-versus-JPEG2000 rate–distortion table was found, and the PNAS collage-theorem year (1985 vs 1986) needs checking.

**What this means for the paper.** A paper on fractal optimisation of tensor landscapes can honestly say that the trainability boundary in hyperparameter space is empirically fractal (dimension 1.2–2.0 in width-16 MLPs), that SGD paths show anomalous diffusion with self-affine fractal geometry (D_f 1.3–2.7; Hausdorff ≈ 2.7–3.1 under an fBm model), and that constant-step SGD behaves like an OU process with covariance ∝ lr/batch under a quadratic, Gaussian-noise, small-step approximation, which licenses "schedule as annealing" as an analogy. On speed it can say that every known asymptotic gain from scale separation is conditional on a specific provable regularity and that self-similar geometry is cheap to instance. The one sentence no source supports is "recursion or self-similarity makes arbitrary shapes or arbitrary optimisation faster"; a bridge from fractal landscape geometry to an asymptotic optimisation speedup would have to be stated as the paper's own hypothesis.

## 5. Synthetic Silviculture: the loop a browser version must contain

### The unit of self-organisation is the module, and the bud-scale rules come from 2009

A framing correction first: Synthetic Silviculture does not simulate buds or internodes. Its unit is the *branch module*, a small pre-designed branching graph, and the plant is "represented as a collection of branch modules (plant scale)" while "an ecosystem is composed of a number of plants (ecosystem scale)"; development is deterministic within a module (positions, diameters and lengths interpolated from initial to final) and self-organising at plant scale (modules added or shed by vigor) ([Makowski et al. 2019](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)). The bud-fate machinery, the voxel shadow pyramid, Q = max(0, C − s + a), n = ⌊v⌋ metamers, the tropism weighted sum and light/size shedding all come from the self-organising tree paper it builds on, which it re-uses at module scale ([Pałubicki et al. 2009](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.small.pdf)); both are given here so the reimplementation can choose its level.

At bud scale the 2009 loop is: shadow propagation, where a bud in voxel (I,J,K) casts a pyramid downward into (I ± p, J − q, K ± p) for q = 0..q_max, p = 0..q, each voxel receiving Δs = a·b^{−q}, and light exposure is **Q = max(C − s + a, 0)** (the +a means a bud does not shade itself) with the optimal growth direction the negative shadow gradient; typical grid 200³, cell edge ≈ one internode, q_max 4–8. Then the extended Borchert–Honda allocation: a basipetal pass sums Q to the base, v_base = α·Q_base, and an acropetal pass splits at each branching point **v_m = v·λQ_m/(λQ_m + (1−λ)Q_l), v_l = v·(1−λ)Q_l/(λQ_m + (1−λ)Q_l)**, with λ > 0.5 excurrent (trunked), λ < 0.5 decurrent (bushy); Figure 7 spans λ = 0.46…0.54 at α = 2. A bud receiving v grows **n = ⌊v⌋ metamers of length v/n**; metamer orientation is a weighted sum of default direction, the light direction (weight ξ) and a tropism vector (weight η); widths follow the pipe model d^n = d1^n + d2^n with n ≈ 2–3 and never shrink; branches whose gathered light per internode falls below a threshold are shed (after Takenaka 1994), which is what forms tall boles. Space colonisation is the alternative environment (θ ≈ 90°, ρ = 2, r = 4–6 internode lengths) but is "about three times slower" and its binary Q breaks shedding ([Pałubicki et al. 2009](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.small.pdf)). Borchert & Honda's original rule splits flux as F_{2i} = F_i·R_f to the main daughter, with R_f = 0.8 for laterals in their asymmetric runs and a terminal below F_min surviving N_s increments before dying ([Borchert & Honda 1984](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/BorchertHonda1984.pdf)).

At module scale the 2019 paper replaces the voxel read with a continuous overlap: each module u gets a bounding sphere B_u, **f_collisions(u) = Σ_w V_intersect(B_u, B_w)** and **Q(u) = exp(−f_collisions(u))** (Eq. 1); the BH passes are identical, v̄(u_l) = v̄(u) − v̄(u_m), and the root never exceeds v̄_rootmax (Eq. 2). Resource becomes a growth *rate* via a smoothstep, **Υ(u) = S((v̄ − v̄_min)/(v̄_max − v̄_min))·g_p** with S(x) = 3x² − 2x³ (Eq. 5), integrated forward-Euler into a physiological age da/dt = Υ until a_mature (Eq. 6); a mature module hands q = Q(u)/#terminals to each terminal node, runs BH again, and attaches a new module wherever v > v̄_min. Segment age is a_b = max(0, a_u − a_n), diameter d_b = √(Σ d_c²) (pipe model, n = 2) or φ at tips, length ℓ_b = min(ℓ_max, β·a_b) (Eqs. 7–9); tropism is a positional offset **τ(a_b) = g1·g_dir·g2/(a_b + g1)** (Eq. 10). New modules are oriented by minimising ω1·f_collisions + ω2·f_tropism over the four Euler perturbations {[±α,0,0],[0,0,±α]}, converging in about three steps. A module is shed when v̄ < v̄_min. Module *type* is picked from nine prototypes (after Hallé et al. 1978) laid out in a Voronoi-partitioned morphospace of apical control λ and determinacy D, at (λ, D′) with D′ = v̄(parent)·D/v̄_max, so vigorous parents spawn more deterministic (monopodial) modules; at flowering age F_eff = F_age·v̄_rootmax/v̄_root the species switches to λ_mature, D_mature ([Makowski et al. 2019](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)).

### The ecosystem loop: shared shadow, Gaussian seeding, age-driven senescence, climate as a scalar

Competition has two ranges. Locally, overlapping bounding spheres reduce Q and the orientation search steers new modules away from collisions; at distance, a uniform grid is updated so that whenever a module is associated with a cell, light availability Q_G of that cell "and all cells underneath" drops (the shadow extends to the bottom, ≈ 2 m), and the effective exposure becomes **Q_eff = lerp(s_tol, 1, Q·Q_G)** with shade tolerance s_tol ∈ [0,1]. Seeding begins at flowering age: new plants are placed periodically (seeding frequency, 1–10 per period, period 1/year) in a circular area (seeding radius, 0.01–100 m) with positions drawn from a Gaussian. Death comes from age, when p_t ≥ p_max and v̄_rootmax is "linearly interpolated to zero" so every module eventually starves below v̄_min and is shed, or from starvation under a canopy; uniformly aged stands die together as cohort senescence, producing gaps. Climate enters only as a scalar: temperature falls linearly with elevation, T(h) = T(0) + γh, precipitation is constant, and per-species adaptation **o = N_T(T)·N_P(P)/(N_T(T_A)·N_P(P_A))** (Eq. 11) linearly scales v̄_rootmax and seeding frequency, so a poorly adapted species "might not grow at all or at a very slow rate". Terrain is a height map plus a binary soil mask; there is no slope factor. Nine biomes sit on a Whittaker diagram and species are hand-placed by T_A, P_A after Bassuk et al. 2009. The whole parameter space is "2 environmental, 5 plasticity parameters" ([Makowski et al. 2019](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)). Published plasticity values (Table 3): the Fig. 7 demos use seeding frequency 3.0, radius 1.0 or 9.0, s_tol 0.9 or 0.1, T_A 28.0, P_A 4100; the Fig. 19 landscape uses shrub (5.0, 8.0, 0.15, T_A 8.0, P_A 672), conifer (2.0, 3.0, 0.45, 12.0, 672) and deciduous (1.0, 3.0, 0.6, 16.0, 672). Unpublished: the variances of N_T and N_P, how the seeding radius maps to σ, any minimum establishment distance or seedling mortality beyond starvation.

The validation that the loop produces ecology rather than decoration: 1,000 pines' total biomass follows a logistic with L = 3000, k = 0.0625, x0 = 120; density against trunk diameter follows the 3/2 self-thinning law; height–diameter and leaf-mass–diameter allometries agree with Eloy et al. 2017; module overlap stays under 5% with orientation optimisation; a three-species succession over 500 years ends with the shade-tolerant conifer as climax; removing plants triggers 150-year gap dynamics with fast palms then the climax deciduous ([Makowski et al. 2019, §8, Figs. 3, 10, 14–16](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)). The researchers' pseudo-code faithful to the paper, per yearly step: rebuild the shadow grid from module positions; per plant compute Q per module (sphere overlap × grid), run both BH passes, compute growth rates, age modules, attach and shed modules, orient new ones; per plant, if age ≥ p_max ramp v̄_rootmax toward zero and remove the plant when no modules remain; per mature plant, with probability ∝ o·seedingFrequency, place seeds at Gaussian(σ = seedingRadius) around it, rejecting blocked cells; T(h) and o are static per cell and precomputable.

### Multi-scale means module / plant / ecosystem, rendered by instancing, with no LOD

The "multi-scale" of the title is the three tiers, not a coarse-versus-detailed plant switch. "Unlike previous approaches, where branch patches are used to represent the full surface geometry of branch parts, our modules only represent the topology", and because a module's topology comes from one of nine shared prototypes, "the use of branch modules allows for GPU instancing … while keeping a very light memory and performance footprint"; each node stores position, physiological age, branch length and thickening factor φ, and geometry is built in vertex/geometry/fragment shaders as generalised cylinders, foliage as textured quads, with export limited to skeletal graphs plus transforms, connectivity, diameter and plant type. The same tree at ~200 K polygons can be built from 99 complex, 294, or 1204 simple modules, which the authors call "a compression scheme"; level-of-detail techniques are listed as future work ([Makowski et al. 2019, §3, §7, §8, §9](https://wp.faculty.wmi.amu.edu.pl/papers/Makowski.etal-2019-Synthetic-Silviculture.pdf)). The 2009 trees, by contrast, carry 1,000–700,000 metamers each ([Pałubicki et al. 2009](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.small.pdf)).

### No official code, one documented port, no browser version

Neither paper released code: the project page lists paper, glossary supplement and video only ([project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/synthetic_silviculture/index.html)), the SIGGRAPH 2019 replicability tracker lists it with video and no code ([replicability.graphics #75](https://github.com/GraphicsReplicability/replicability.graphics/issues/75)). The most useful artefact is **wlejon/broflora**, a C++20 MIT port implementing all three tiers, "extended Borchert-Honda pass with basipetal and acropetal sweeps", pipe-model thickening, "spatial light and shadow grids updated each step", prototype-Voronoi spawning, climate seeding and senescence, with three built-in prototypes (`straightModule`, `forkModule`, `whorlModule`) and a JS binding `bro.flora` inside the author's engine ([GitHub](https://github.com/wlejon/broflora)); its strategy document is a line-by-line checklist that flags every under-specification in the paper and states its own choices: shadow stamp 1 − exp(−k r²) per module instead of a beam integration, O(N²) sphere overlap, an N-child vigor split ("mainline gets λ·Q_main, laterals share (1−λ)·Q_lat weighted by their own subtree light"), coordinate-descent orientation (8 iterations, halving step), seeding probability σ·min(1, dt) per tick, cascading sheds, and slope-gated seeding added "beyond the paper" ([broflora strategy doc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wlejon/broflora/main/docs/auto-flora-strategy.md)). Also present: a Rust port with a one-line README ([hindlet](https://github.com/hindlet/synthetic_silviculture)), a C++ student project ([ndevlin](https://github.com/ndevlin/EcosystemCreatorRepo)), a CUDA wildfire project using the module representation ([anthonymansur](https://github.com/anthonymansur/CIS565-Final-Project)). For the 2009 model: what appears to be the original LPFG L+C/C++ code ([inuritdino/SOT](https://github.com/inuritdino/SOT), provenance unstated), a BSD C++/OpenGL port ([bernardosulzbach](https://github.com/bernardosulzbach/self-organizing-tree-models)), and a Unity exercise from Pałubicki's own department implementing the downward-widening shadow stamp ([AMU MWS cw5](https://git.wmi.amu.edu.pl/s439476/MWS/compare/master...cw5)). Space colonisation has mature JS ports ([npm space-colonization](https://npmjs.com/package/space-colonization); [jasonwebb 2-D experiments](https://github.com/jasonwebb/2d-space-colonization-experiments)). No JS/WebGL port of shadow propagation or of Synthetic Silviculture was found. Related work verified: Plastic Trees ([Pirk et al. 2012](https://cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/Pirk12ToG)), inverse procedural modelling whose 25-parameter trees the 2019 paper decomposes into 10 modules and regrows ([Stava et al. 2014](https://diglib.eg.org/items/4c6abd53-959e-4336-af65-3eb719b9c1c3)), and a GPU (OpenCL) version of the self-organising pipeline by Kohek & Strnad known only by catalogue record ([COBISS](https://plus.cobiss.net/cobiss/si/en/bib/18066454)).

**What this means for the paper.** A browser demo earns the name only if it contains the ecosystem loop, not just a handsome tree: many plants competing for light through a shared shadow field (the a·b^{−q} pyramid or the column lerp(s_tol, 1, Q·Q_G)), Borchert–Honda allocation with a per-species λ, shedding of starved branches, per-species p_max with senescence, and Gaussian-radius seeding, so that succession, gap dynamics and 3/2 self-thinning emerge. What can be dropped without betraying the model: the nine-prototype morphospace and its orientation search (replace with 2009 bud-level growth, since the ecosystem layer never depends on modules), terrain beyond a flat plane or one elevation→temperature gradient, soil beyond a mask, and the climate Gaussian, flowering switch and LOD as optional decoration; keep one or two species differing in λ, s_tol, p_max and seeding radius, a 2-D column map or 64³ grid, and instanced rendering.

## Conclusion

Read together, the five tracks share one shape. In each, a widely repeated slogan dissolves into a precise statement that is both narrower and more usable. "Diffusion is Brownian" becomes "additive isotropic Gaussian noise at every level, with Tweedie's denoiser", which is what the spectral argument needs and holds for SD3 and FLUX where the Brownian path does not. "Generation order follows the spectrum" becomes a theorem for Gaussian fields that nobody has bothered to write down, plus an ensemble regularity for photographs with a measurable spread, and Falck's EqualSNR result shows the hierarchy is a consequence of white noise meeting a power law rather than a precondition for quality. "Diffusion LMs plan the structure first" is contradicted by the only trajectory measurements, while the scale-free memory of text (H ≈ 0.7) is real and is the right target for the experiment that would make the claim. "Loss landscapes are fractal" is supported for a stability boundary in hyperparameter space and for optimiser paths, not for the surface, and "self-similarity makes things faster" is true exactly when a specific regularity is provable and never otherwise. Synthetic Silviculture, finally, is a model whose published formulas are complete enough to port and whose unpublished constants are few enough to guess.

The practical implication for a paper series is that the theorems iori wants mostly do not exist yet in the form wanted, and the ones that do (Guth's conditioning bound, Hunt's scale-equivariant filter, Lin & Tegmark's grammar result, Davis's Haar equivalence, the collage theorem) each prove something adjacent. That leaves room for the papers to supply the missing links as their own contributions, provided they are labelled as such: the ordering theorem for Gaussian fields from the Wiener gain plus Anderson reversal, the scale-relative schedule coordinate that reconciles Guth, Hunt and simple diffusion, a commit-order-versus-discourse-depth measurement for diffusion LMs, and a hypothesis, not a result, linking fractal landscape geometry to speed.
