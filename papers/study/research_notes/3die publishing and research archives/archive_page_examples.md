# Minimalist research-archive and publication index pages (Nov 2026 survey)

Method note: every site below was fetched live on 2026-11-09 with a text-extracting fetcher. The fetcher sees structure and text, not rendered styling, so typography and colour are only described where the text shows them (heading levels, symbols, labels) or where the site documents its own design (gwern /design, Tufte CSS, brutalist-web.design). Sites that blocked the fetcher (403/429/DNS) are listed under Gaps and are not described from memory.

## How do these sites present their archive or index? (catalogue of examples)

### Takeaway
Of 27 attempted sites, 21 rendered and are described here. Almost all fall into five shapes: (1) a single reverse-chronological list (one line or card per entry), (2) an outline of headings, each holding a list, (3) numbered issues, (4) a card grid with filter tabs, (5) no index at all, entered through links (Matuschak). The archives that read as "design-led" are mostly shapes 1 and 2, with very little metadata per entry.

### Cited Findings

**Shape 1: one reverse-chronological list**
- Distill: one vertical list of article cards (thumbnail, title, authors, one-line summary), newest first, with no filters. Metadata per entry: publication date, an optional "Peer-reviewed" label that links to the public review on GitHub, and a category label (Editorial / Commentary / Thread) where one applies. The index shows no PDF or code links. Footer: About, Submit, Prize, Archive, RSS, GitHub, ISSN 2476-0757. ARCHIVED IN PRACTICE: the "Distill Hiatus" editorial is dated 2 July 2021, and the newest articles are dated 2 September 2021 — [distill.pub](https://distill.pub/)
- Distill's journal page: registered with the Library of Congress and CrossRef, one journal-level DOI prefix (10.23915/distill), indexed in Google Scholar. Articles are HTML built on Distill's infrastructure, and interactivity is common but optional. Review continues after publication through GitHub issues, and serious unresolved issues can be shown in the article margin. On publication the article repo becomes public under the distillpub GitHub org for preservation. The page describes no formal errata or versioning process — [distill.pub/journal](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- Ink & Switch essay index: one reverse-chronological list. Each entry has a linked title, a one-line description, a small research-area icon, a type label ("Essay" or "Opinion") and only a year. No numbers, full dates, authors or PDFs appear in the index. The home page's "Featured Work" mixes essays with undated "Lab Notebook" entries — [inkandswitch.com/essay](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/); [inkandswitch.com](https://www.inkandswitch.com/)
- Craig Mod, essays: one vertical list, newest first. Each block holds a month and year, a title, and a smaller one-line subtitle at a lower heading level. There are no year headings, and outlets are named once in an intro paragraph instead of on each entry. A separate "Popular Essays" list shows titles and subtitles without dates — [craigmod.com/essays](https://craigmod.com/essays/)
- The Pudding archive: a vertical list of cards numbered #228 down to #202 (a running story number). Each card has a thumbnail, month and year, a bold title and a one-sentence description. Filter tabs: All, Our Faves, Popular, Updating, Your Input, Video, Audio. Paging uses "Load More Stories". Promotional blocks (merch, Patreon, newsletter) sit between entries, and cards show no authors — [pudding.cool/archives](https://pudding.cool/archives/)
- Quanta archive: one column, newest first. Each entry has a thumbnail, a topic tag, headline, author, date, comment link, Save/Read Later and a summary. A sidebar holds a topic filter and "Most Read". Numbered pagination runs 1–264 — [quantamagazine.org/archive](https://www.quantamagazine.org/archive/)
- Public Domain Review essays: a vertical list. Each entry has a thumbnail, title, summary with "more", the author (linked to a contributors page), an exact date (for example 28 Oct 2026) and subject tags. A "Browse by Subject" panel offers 12 categories, and pages are numbered — [publicdomainreview.org/essays](https://publicdomainreview.org/essays/)
- Google DeepMind publications: 267 entries, each showing only a date and a title, newest first, with nine pages, no filters and no authors or venues in the listing — [deepmind.google/research/publications](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/)
- arXiv listing (cs.GR/recent): a numbered list grouped under date headings that give a daily count (for example "Mon, 9 Nov 2026"). Each entry has an ID linking to the abstract, plus pdf / html / other-formats links, title, linked authors, free-text Comments (page count, venue, code link) and subjects, with the primary subject first and cross-lists marked. The version (v1, v2) shows only in the HTML link — [arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent)

**Shape 2: outline of headings, each holding a list**
- Bret Victor (worrydream.com): one text column with no nav bar. Groups use short, often lowercase labels ("placemaking", "Earlier work.", "Bits & blurts.", "Realtoys.", "Poems & diversions."). Inside each group, year subheadings (for example "2024") run newest first. Each item is a bulleted link followed by a short descriptor. Secondary formats sit in parentheses or after "&" (talk, notes, poster). Year ranges such as "(2011-2022)" mark long projects. Uncaptioned preview images sit near the bottom — [worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/)
- gwern.net index: a categorised directory. A top block points to About / Design / Changelog / feeds / newsletter and explains the toolbar. It is followed by "Newest" (recently modified), "Popular" and "Notable" lists, a transcluded feed of the 29 most recent blog posts, then topic sections separated by rules. Entries are mostly bare linked titles. Hover popups show the full title, author, year and an extract. A star marks new pages. A page can appear in several sections — [gwern.net/index](https://gwern.net/index)
- Robin Sloan: the home page is a short bio, then sections split by "* * *" rules and introduced by bold links (newsletter "every 29 ½ days", blog). The archive is an outline (Basics, Archives, Novels, Novellas, Special projects, Short stories, Notable essays) of bulleted titles with no dates. Pieces published elsewhere carry the outlet name. A ★ marks some items without explanation. The one date on the page is a site-wide "last updated" (8 Nov 2026). The text uses curly apostrophes, em dashes and soft hyphens — [robinsloan.com](https://www.robinsloan.com/)
- UbuWeb: a large wordmark over a horizontal row of section links (Film & Video, Sound, Dance, Papers, Historical, Visual Poetry, … /ubu Editions, Top Tens). It still describes itself as founded in 1996 and says the Library of Congress archives it permanently — [ubu.com](https://www.ubu.com/)
- Nicky Case: the home page groups work into play / read / watch sections. "SHTUFF YOU CAN PLAY" shows six thumbnail cards, each with a title and a dash-separated one-line description, plus a "(see all projects)" link. The cards show no dates, play time or translation counts — [ncase.me](https://ncase.me/)
- Stripe Press: the whole catalogue is one long vertical page (the fetched text ran past 500k characters). Each book has a large title, subtitle, author, blurb, bio, praise quotes, price and retailer buttons. Some add a "Living cover" page, a sample, or a downloadable PDF zine. There are no series groupings, author bios sit inside the entries rather than on separate pages, and years and formats are not shown consistently — [press.stripe.com](https://press.stripe.com/)

**Shape 3: numbered issues**
- Asterisk "All Issues": Issue 16 (Oct 2026) back to Issue 01 (Nov 2022), newest first. Each issue has a two-digit number, a one-word or short theme (Science, Mistakes, Food, Church & State), month and year, a cover thumbnail and a short topic teaser. Article lists and contributors live under separate Articles and Contributors nav items — [asteriskmag.com/issues](https://asteriskmag.com/issues)
- Works in Progress: issues 26 down to 03, plus "Special Issue 01" (Oct 2022), at /issue-NN/. Articles show the title, "Words by" with linked authors, a summary and a topic tag, with no reading time or per-article date. Print is sold as six issues a year at $100, and Issue 27 ships in early December. There is a separate /archive/ and /topic/ pages. NOTE: /issues/ returns 404, so the archive moved to /archive/ — [worksinprogress.co](https://worksinprogress.co/)
- e-flux journal: the landing page shows only the current issue, #166 (October 2026), with a captioned cover image (a public-domain Picabia detail). It lists an Editorial first, then essays (title + author). Multi-part essays are labelled "Part 1". Per-essay dates are not shown, and the URL carries the issue number. The page offers no visible full #1–#N index, filters or PDFs — [e-flux.com/journal](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/)
- Kernel (Reboot): past issues appear as cards (cover, issue number, theme posed as a question such as "What are the odds?", one-line description). Issues 1–4 are archived, and Issue 5 is current. The footer groups issues by year. Articles are grouped by form (Nonfiction / Fiction / Poetry / Experimental & Multimedia) with a matching filter bar. Fiction and poetry entries show only title and author. NOTE: kernel-magazine.com does not resolve; the live domain is kernelmag.io — [kernelmag.io](https://kernelmag.io/)

**Shape 4: card grid with filter tabs**
- Maggie Appleton garden: 168 entries as cards (optional image, title, summary, type, relative date such as "about 1 year ago"). Types: Essays, Notes, Patterns, Smidgeons, Talks, Podcasts, Library, Antilibrary, Now. Growth stages: Seedling / Budding / Evergreen. Three filters (about 35 topics, stage, type) show counts — [maggieappleton.com/garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden)
- Anthropic research: a gallery of 207 posts, "Most recent" by default, with a Filter control and a Gallery view toggle. A five-item featured carousel sits at the top, and team cards show post counts (Alignment 51, Frontier Red Team 38). Each entry has a thumbnail, team label, date, title and summary, with no authors and no PDF links in the listing. Paging uses "See more" — [anthropic.com/research](https://www.anthropic.com/research)
- Sakana AI: a "Selected work" card grid. Each card has an image, a venue-and-year line (for example "ICLR 2026"), a topic label (RSI / FIG), title, summary, and sometimes an "Open source" badge. Cards link to Sakana's own pages, not to arXiv or code. An English / 日本語 toggle maps to /ja/ — [sakana.ai](https://sakana.ai/)
- Are.na Editorial: a vertical stack, each entry with a category label ("Essay"), title, excerpt, date and author. "Annual" books appear only in site navigation. CAUTION: the capture showed placeholder text, so real entries were not verified — [are.na/editorial](https://www.are.na/editorial)

**Shape 5: no index**
- Andy Matuschak's notes: the site says it deliberately has no index or navigational aids. Readers start from a suggested entry note ("What's top of mind") and follow inline links — [notes.andymatuschak.org](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/)

**Reference style guides**
- Tufte CSS: sidenotes (numbered) and margin notes (symbol) beside the text, which collapse behind a toggle on small screens. Off-white background, off-black text, ET Book with serif fallbacks, and underlined (not coloured) links. Figures can go in the main column, the margin or full width. It is a starting point, not a framework ("The web is not print") — [edwardtufte.github.io/tufte-css](https://edwardtufte.github.io/tufte-css/)
- Brutalist Web Design: seven rules. Readable on any device and screen reader, only links and buttons react to clicks, links underlined and buttons native, the back button works, scrolling instead of pagination, decoration only when it serves the content, speed as a feature — [brutalist-web.design](https://brutalist-web.design/)
- gwern /design: every page has a metadata block (created date, last major edit, status such as notes / in progress / finished, a confidence rating, importance 0–10). Pages are static (Pandoc + Hakyll). Reading works without JS, and popups, transclusion, sortable tables and sidenotes are layered on top. External links are mirrored locally against link rot. Readers get light / dark / auto themes and a reader mode. The palette is greyscale — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design)

### Inferences
- Distill is the closest precedent for "long interactive web papers with live figures", but it has been frozen since 2021. It is a model for article format and citation infrastructure (ISSN, DOI, Scholar, public review on GitHub), not for a living index.
- Bret Victor and Robin Sloan are the closest precedents for an artist's body of mixed work: grouped by kind, then by year, with one descriptor and secondary formats inline.

### Gaps
- Blocked or unavailable to the fetcher: MIT Press Reader (403), Monoskop (403 on /log and the wiki), Primary Information (403), Observable (429). Not attempted within the budget: OpenAI research index, Google Research blog, Explorable Explanations hub (explorabl.es), Ted Nelson / Xanadu (xanadu.com). None of these are described here, to avoid describing remembered rather than current designs.
- Visual typography (typefaces, sizes, colours) was not observable through text fetches except where sites document it.

## Which examples handle a numbered series well?

### Takeaway
Magazines number issues plainly and pair each number with a theme (Asterisk "Issue 13 — Science", Kernel "Issue 3 — question", e-flux "#166"). Running story numbers (Pudding #228) and arXiv IDs are the other working models. None of the sites checked uses decimal sub-numbering such as 0.1 / 0.2. That idea would be ours.

### Cited Findings
- Asterisk: a zero-padded two-digit issue number, a theme word and a month/year, newest first. The archive is the list of numbers — [asteriskmag.com/issues](https://asteriskmag.com/issues)
- Works in Progress keeps a separate "Special Issue 01" track beside the main issue numbers and uses /issue-NN/ URLs — [worksinprogress.co](https://worksinprogress.co/)
- Kernel: the theme of each issue is a question, and the footer groups issues by year — [kernelmag.io](https://kernelmag.io/)
- e-flux: the issue number is in the URL, and "Part 1" labels mark essays serialised across issues — [e-flux.com/journal](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/)
- The Pudding: every story carries a running number (#228…) in the archive — [pudding.cool/archives](https://pudding.cool/archives/)
- arXiv: the ID encodes year and month, and the version appears as a v1/v2 suffix — [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent)

### Inferences
- For "0.1, 0.2, 0.3" papers hanging off paper 0, the nearest working pattern is Works in Progress's parallel "Special Issue" track plus e-flux's "Part N" labels. A tree (0 → 0.1, 0.2, 0.3) would be novel among these sites. It suits an indented outline (Victor / Sloan style) better than a card grid.

### Gaps
- No example of a decimal-numbered paper series was found among the fetched sites.

## Which show interactivity status, reading time, version history, errata, changelogs or "last updated" well?

### Takeaway
Few index pages show any of these. gwern is the strong case: per-page created / modified / status / confidence / importance, a site Changelog and a "Newest" list of modified pages. Maggie Appleton's growth stages are a lighter version of the same idea. Distill's public, post-publication review on GitHub is the nearest thing to an errata channel. The Pudding's "Updating" tab is the only interactivity / live-status filter found.

### Cited Findings
- gwern: metadata block on every page, star for new pages, a "Newest" list marked as recently modified, a Changelog — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design); [gwern.net/index](https://gwern.net/index)
- Maggie Appleton: Seedling / Budding / Evergreen stages that can be filtered, and relative dates — [maggieappleton.com/garden](https://maggieappleton.com/garden)
- Distill: a "Peer-reviewed" label linking to the review, post-publication issues on GitHub, and serious unresolved ones shown in the article margin. No versioning process is described — [distill.pub](https://distill.pub/); [distill.pub/journal](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- The Pudding: an "Updating" filter tab for stories that keep changing, plus Video / Audio tabs for medium — [pudding.cool/archives](https://pudding.cool/archives/)
- Robin Sloan: one site-wide "last updated" date — [robinsloan.com](https://www.robinsloan.com/)
- arXiv: the version is visible only in the HTML link suffix — [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent)
- Works in Progress and Asterisk show no reading time on archive entries — [worksinprogress.co](https://worksinprogress.co/); [asteriskmag.com/issues](https://asteriskmag.com/issues)

### Inferences
- An imprint of living interactive papers has room to stand out here: a per-entry "first published / last revised (commit)" pair plus a status word would cover version and errata at almost no cost. The repo already links paper 0.3's first-published commit in its footer.

### Gaps
- No site seen shows reading time on the index. No site seen marks "this entry contains live figures / needs WebGPU" on the index.

## Which handle multiple authors and contributor pages?

### Takeaway
Magazines and Distill list authors on each entry and link them to contributor pages. Lab indexes (Anthropic, DeepMind, Ink & Switch, Sakana) list no authors at all at index level.

### Cited Findings
- Public Domain Review: author linked to a contributors page on every entry — [publicdomainreview.org/essays](https://publicdomainreview.org/essays/)
- Works in Progress: "Words by" with linked author names — [worksinprogress.co](https://worksinprogress.co/)
- Asterisk: a separate Contributors section in the nav — [asteriskmag.com/issues](https://asteriskmag.com/issues)
- Distill: authors on each card — [distill.pub](https://distill.pub/)
- arXiv: every author name is linked (to an author search) — [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent)
- No authors in the listing at Anthropic research, DeepMind publications or the Ink & Switch essay index — [anthropic.com/research](https://www.anthropic.com/research); [deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/); [inkandswitch.com/essay](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/)
- Stripe Press puts author bios inside each book entry, with no separate author pages — [press.stripe.com](https://press.stripe.com/)

### Inferences
- For a label imprint with several artists, the PDR / Works in Progress pattern (byline linked to a contributor page) fits naturally and can reuse the existing 3die.fr artist pages as the contributor pages.

### Gaps
- How each site builds its contributor page was not fetched.

## Which minimal approaches load fast and work as plain HTML, accessible and print-friendly?

### Takeaway
The text-first outline pages (worrydream, Robin Sloan, Craig Mod's list, UbuWeb's link row, arXiv) are essentially plain HTML lists. gwern and Tufte CSS show how to add sidenotes and popups as progressive enhancement. Brutalist Web Design gives the checklist.

### Cited Findings
- gwern: static compiled pages, reading works without JS, enhancements layered on top, reader mode, dark mode, mirrored links — [gwern.net/design](https://gwern.net/design)
- Tufte CSS: plain CSS, underlined links, sidenotes that collapse on small screens — [Tufte CSS](https://edwardtufte.github.io/tufte-css/)
- Brutalist Web Design rules: readable everywhere including screen readers, a working back button, scrolling instead of pagination, speed — [brutalist-web.design](https://brutalist-web.design/)
- Opposite cases: "Load More" buttons (Pudding, Anthropic), numbered pagination (Quanta 1–264, PDR, DeepMind) and carousels (Anthropic) — [pudding.cool](https://pudding.cool/archives/); [anthropic.com/research](https://www.anthropic.com/research); [quantamagazine.org](https://www.quantamagazine.org/archive/)

### Inferences
- An archive of maybe 10–50 papers needs no pagination, filters or JS. One HTML list, with a print stylesheet, meets every brutalist rule.

### Gaps
- Page weight and Lighthouse scores were not measured.

## What do the best minimal archives leave out? (recurring patterns and anti-patterns)

### Takeaway
They leave out filters, pagination, thumbnails, author lists on lab pages, reading time, tags, exact dates (a year or month is enough) and, in Matuschak's case, the index itself. What they keep is a title, one line of description and a date or group heading.

### Cited Findings
- Year-only dating: Ink & Switch; month and year: Craig Mod, Pudding, Asterisk; no dates: Sloan, Nicky Case — [inkandswitch.com/essay](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/); [craigmod.com/essays](https://craigmod.com/essays/); [robinsloan.com](https://www.robinsloan.com/); [ncase.me](https://ncase.me/)
- Title plus a one-line descriptor is near universal: worrydream, Ink & Switch, Craig Mod (subtitle), Nicky Case, Distill — sources as above
- Secondary formats are written inline as words (talk, notes, poster) instead of icons: worrydream — [worrydream.com](https://worrydream.com/)
- Only Monoskop (unverified) and arXiv put PDF / HTML / code links in the index. Distill, Anthropic and Sakana keep them on the article page — [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.GR/recent); [distill.pub](https://distill.pub/); [sakana.ai](https://sakana.ai/)
- Anti-patterns seen: promotional blocks inside the archive list (Pudding), unexplained symbols (Sloan's ★), relative dates that go stale ("about 1 year ago", Appleton), a current-issue-only landing with no visible back index (e-flux), dead archive URLs (worksinprogress /issues 404), domain drift (kernel-magazine.com → kernelmag.io), placeholder content in the live markup (Are.na capture) — sources as cited above

### Inferences
- Recurring pattern for a design-led imprint: a mono-column outline with numbered entries, title, a one-line descriptor, a short date, and formats as plain words (web · pdf · code · data). Distill-grade citation metadata and gwern-style status and revision live on the paper page, not the index.
- Since nothing seen handles decimal series numbering or live-figure / interactivity status, "design our own" on top of the Victor / Sloan outline pattern looks justified over adopting any one site's system.

### Gaps
- Whether the frozen Distill template (distillpub/template) still builds in 2026 was not checked.
