# Imprint models for "3die publishing": how labels, collectives and small presses publish their members' texts

Research date: 9 November 2026. 18 web searches, no page fetches; most findings come from search-result summaries of primary pages (press about pages, Wikipedia, trade press). Anything stated from background knowledge and not checked in this pass is placed in Inferences or Gaps and labelled "unverified".

## Which labels and collectives run a publishing arm or text series, and are they active in 2026?

### Takeaway
The closest precedent is **Shelter Press** (Rennes, FR): one house that puts out records and books, with a numbered book series, *Spectres*. Next come US nonprofits **Blank Forms** and **Primary Information**, which link archival or artist publishing to concerts and free PDFs. **Urbanomic**, an arts organisation turned press, shows how a small house can reach world distribution through MIT Press. Most record labels do not run their own book arm. Their founders (Kode9 for one) publish with outside presses.

### Cited Findings
- **Shelter Press** is a French independent label and publisher of records and books. Publisher Bartolomé Sanson and sound artist Félicia Atkinson run it. Sources give 2011 or 2012 as the founding year, and one says it grew out of Kaugummi Books. Its founders: "We are not sellers or product-makers. We are publishers." — [Tiny Mix Tapes](https://tinymixtapes.com/node/141572%3Fver%3Damp); [Bandcamp Daily label profile](https://daily.bandcamp.com/shelter-press-label-profile); [Les presses du réel publisher page](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/editeur.php?id=155)
- Shelter Press also curates art projects and runs multimedia events and exhibitions. Its *Spectres* book series reached vol. III, *Ghosts in the Machine* (26 Nov 2021, 224 pp., EN/FR). Since 2021 it also carries and works on releases for Ideologic Organ, Recollection GRM and Portraits GRM. — [Bleep listing](https://bleep.com/merch/256599-various-artists-spectres-iii-ghosts-in-the-machine); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/shelter-press)
- Les presses du réel (Dijon) distributes Shelter Press books in France and Blank Forms books in Europe. — [Les presses du réel: Shelter Press](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/editeur.php?id=155); [Les presses du réel: Blank Forms](https://lespressesdureel.com/EN/editeur.php?id=257)
- **Blank Forms Editions** is the publishing arm of Blank Forms, a Brooklyn nonprofit that also gives artists curatorial support, residencies and commissions. It publishes a numbered anthology series (vol. 09 *Sound Signatures*, vol. 10 *Alien Roots: Éliane Radigue*), standalone books and reissues (Derek Jarman, Jerry Hunt), records on Bandcamp, and numbered "art editions". A 588-page title is dated 13 Jan 2026, so it is active. D.A.P./Artbook distributes it. — [Artbook D.A.P. Blank Forms](https://www.artbook.com/blank-forms.html); [Blank Forms Editions Bandcamp](https://blankformseditions.bandcamp.com/artists); [blankforms.org art edition 7](https://www.blankforms.org/publications/graham-lambkin-joe-mcphee-the-wolf-has-come-down-from-the-north-art-edition-7)
- **Primary Information** is a Brooklyn nonprofit founded in 2006 by Miriam Katzeff and James Hoff. It publishes artists' books and writings and reprints out-of-print ones. Its website PDFs are "always free", and it describes "degrees of free": free PDFs, free and $0.99 e-books, paper books, handmade limited editions. It is W.A.G.E.-certified and pays artists and staff fairly. The Warhol Foundation has funded it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Information); [Artforum roundtable](https://www.artforum.com/columns/the-year-in-independent-art-publishing-a-roundtable-222530/); [A.R.T. conversation](https://www.artresourcestransfer.org/resources/notes/primary-information-in-conversation-with-a-r-t); [Warhol Foundation grants](https://warholfoundation.org/grants/archive/primary-information)
- **Urbanomic** is a UK publisher and arts organisation registered in Falmouth and directed by Robin Mackay. It runs house series (*Collapse* journal volumes, *Redactions*, *Mono*) and co-publishes with Sequence Press, New York. Its authors include Negarestani, Nick Land, Amanda Beech and Yuk Hui. — [About Urbanomic](https://urbanomic.com/about); [Penguin Random House: Urbanomic Redactions series](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/series/M6L/urbanomic--redactions); [Motto Distribution: Urbanomic](https://www.mottodistribution.com/shop/publishers/urbanomic.html)
- **Kode9 / Hyperdub:** Steve Goodman's *Sonic Warfare* came out with MIT Press (2009). He co-edited *Unsound:Undead* (2019) and *Sonic Faction: Audio Essay as Medium and Method* (2024) with Urbanomic. The searches found no Hyperdub book imprint. — [Wikipedia: Kode9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kode9); [Schweitzer: Sonic Faction](https://www.schweitzer-online.de/en/buch/Barton/Sonic-Faction/9781915103123/A67942503/)
- **Strange Attractor Press** (London) is run by Mark Pilkington and Jamie Sutcliffe. Sources give 2003 or 2004 as the founding year. It handles all publishing work itself. MIT Press has distributed it worldwide since 1 March 2017, now through Penguin Random House Publisher Services, and State51 handles publicity. MIT Press also distributes Semiotext(e), Goldsmiths Press, Sternberg Press, Urbanomic and Sequence Press. — [Strange Attractor about](https://www.strangeattractor.co.uk/about); [Urbanomic: new distribution](https://www.urbanomic.com/announcement-new-distribution/); [Wikipedia: MIT Press](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Press)
- **Zer0 Books / Repeater:** Tariq Goddard and Mark Fisher founded Zer0 in 2009 as an imprint of John Hunt Publishing. They left in 2014 after disputes with the parent and started Repeater under Watkins Media. Watkins bought John Hunt in 2021, which reunited Zer0 with Repeater. Goddard later stepped down from both. In December 2025 a former Zero editor publicly criticised Watkins's management. — [Wikipedia: Repeater Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeater_Books); [Wikipedia: Collective Ink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Ink); [The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/tariq-goddard-steps-down-as-publisher-of-both-zer0-books-and-repeater-books); [Xenogothic, Dec 2025](https://xenogothic.com/2025/12/05/leaving-watkins-a-statement-from-tristam-adams/)
- **Set Margins'** was founded in 2022 by Freek Lomme, who earlier ran Onomatopee (Eindhoven). It is an EU-based international publisher of art, design and creative writing. Artbook/D.A.P. distributes it in North America, Public Knowledge Books in the UK, Perimeter in Australia and Idea Books elsewhere. — [Artbook D.A.P.: Set Margins](https://www.artbook.com/set-margins.html)
- **tranzit.cz** (Prague) runs a publishing house. It publishes translations of feminist thought, theory and prose in its *navigation* series, plus artist monographs, manifestos and anthologies. It posts yearly editorial plans for 2025 and 2026, so it is active. Books are sold through knihy.matterof.art. The tranzitdisplay partnership with **Display** (Association for Research and Collective Practice) ended in 2018, and the two now run separately. — [tranzit.cz: four new titles 2024](https://cz.tranzit.org/en/project/0/2024-09-23/the-tranzit-cz-publishing-house-presents-four-new-titles); [tranzit editorial plan 2026](https://cz.tranzit.org/en/publications/0/publication/editorial-plan-2026); [tranzit online bookshop](https://cz.tranzit.org/en/project/0/2023-10-01/online-bookshop)
- **Éditions B42** (Montreuil) was founded in 2008 by graphic designer Alexandre Dimos. It began by translating design theory not available in French and is known for careful typography and cover experiments. It publishes a foreign-rights list (2025 list posted April 2026), so it is active. — [B42 about](https://editions-b42.com/en/about/); [Pen Online: French independent publishers](https://pen-online.com/culture/in-france-independent-publishers-are-redefining-the-art-book/); [B42 foreign rights 2025](https://editions-b42.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/B42-Foreign-Rights-2025.pdf)
- **Paraguay Press** (Paris) publishes art and writing, for example an Asier Mendizabal anthology and Bouchra Khalili's *The Circle*. — [rile*books: Paraguay Press](https://www.rile.space/publishers/paraguay-press)

### Inferences
- Shelter Press is the strongest model for 3die. One small team, a French base, records and books under one name, a numbered book series (*Spectres*) whose volumes mix many authors, and a French art-book distributor (Les presses du réel) instead of a big trade house.
- When a label wants books without a full press, it typically either co-publishes or distributes through a press that already has trade distribution (Kode9 → Urbanomic and MIT; Urbanomic → MIT) or runs a nonprofit press beside its events (Blank Forms). The Zer0/Repeater history shows the risk of being an imprint inside someone else's company: the founders lost the name in 2014. 3die publishing should stay inside 3die's own legal entity and keep its name and rights.
- Primary Information's "degrees of free" (free PDF next to a paid physical edition) fits iori's open web papers. The free web version is the work. Print and editions are optional objects for sale.

### Gaps
- Not checked in this pass: PAN, Warp, Mute Books, NTS, Boomkat editorial, Ash International, Errant Bodies, Semiotext(e)'s own operations, Sternberg, Spector, Hyphen, Bedford Press, Divided, &Co, Rhizome, The New Centre, Glass Bead, Merve, Goldsmiths Press (beyond its MIT distribution), Uncertain Commons, Labyrint, AVU publishing, PageFive, Kontrapunkt, Allia, Dent-de-Leone. The searches did not reach their status pages, so the report should not state whether they are active in 2026 without checking. Unverified background knowledge only, to be checked: Mute ran a book imprint alongside *Mute* magazine; Bedford Press was the Architectural Association's imprint; Glass Bead is a Paris-based research platform and online journal.
- Shelter Press's own catalogue-number scheme for books versus records was not checked.
- Display's current publishing activity is unconfirmed.

## Open-access and web-native scholarly presses: what they require and how they make works citable

### Takeaway
Small scholar-led OA presses (punctum, meson) charge no fees, use Creative Commons licences, let authors keep copyright, print on demand and deposit in repositories for DOIs and discovery. Web-native venues show two ways to make code-heavy work citable. Distill stopped as a journal in 2021 and pointed authors to self-publishing. JOSS issues a DOI for the paper and requires an archived release with its own Zenodo DOI.

### Cited Findings
- **punctum books** publishes everything under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 by default and also accepts other CC or copyleft licences the author chooses. Its contract is a non-exclusive, irrevocable licence, so authors keep copyright. It charges no book processing charges. The co-directors review first, then the press follows AAUP peer-review best practice and is open to anonymous or open review. Submissions paused in 2025 and were due to reopen in 2026. — [punctum submit](https://punctumbooks.com/submit); [punctum contract docs](https://docs.punctumbooks.com/books/about-our-book-production-process/page/contract); [How to submit](https://docs.punctumbooks.com/books/about-submitting-your-manuscript/page/how-to-submit-your-manuscript-for-review)
- **meson press** (Lüneburg) came out of Leuphana University's Hybrid Publishing Lab (2012–15). It publishes OA under CC BY-SA where possible, prints on demand for worldwide distribution, and offers free digital editions. Its titles are deposited in DataCite/TIB, SSOAR and DOAB, and its catalogue can be harvested over OAI-PMH. Co-founder Mercedes Bunz has said that NC licences are not really open. — [meson about](https://meson.press/about/); [We are committed to Open Access](https://meson.press/we-are-committed-to-open-access/); [Bunz talk PDF, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7799415/files/meson%20press%20(M%20Bunz).pdf)
- meson belongs to the scholar-led press networks ScholarLed and the Open Book Collective, which funds presses through library memberships. — [Open Book Collective: meson](https://openbookcollective.org/view/initiative/8/moreinfo/); [ScholarLed catalogue](https://simonxix.github.io/scholarled_catalogue/all_press.nbconvert.html)
- **Distill** was an interactive ML journal (2017–2021) edited by Shan Carter, Chris Olah and Arvind Satyanarayan. On 2 July 2021 it went on hiatus because of volunteer burnout. The editors were no longer sure a journal made more sense than encouraging self-publication, and they told authors to keep using the Distill template and self-publish or submit to venues such as VISxAI. No restart was found. — [Distill Hiatus](https://distill.pub/2021/distill-hiatus/); [Wikipedia: Distill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distill_(journal)); [Nature blog 2018](https://blogs.nature.com/naturejobs/2018/04/16/machine-learning-gets-a-journal-for-interactive-figures/)
- **JOSS** reviews the software itself (functionality, docs, tests, licence). The paper is short: authors and affiliations, a statement of need, key references and a link to the archive. Once review is settled, the author tags a release and archives it on Zenodo or figshare. Editors check that the archive's title and authors match the paper, and JOSS then issues its own Crossref DOI. The software and the paper each get a DOI, and ORCID iDs are encouraged. — [JOSS review criteria](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/review_criteria.html); [JOSS editorial guide](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/editing.html); [JOSS design paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7340488/)
- **Ink & Switch** publishes long web essays with a PDF copy. Its *Local-first software* essay appeared on the web and was also presented at Onward! 2019, and Cambridge's repository holds it. No licence statement for the essays was found. — [Ink & Switch local-first](https://inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/); [Kleppmann: local-first at Onward!](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2019/10/23/local-first-at-onward.html); [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/297581)

### Inferences
- For iori's web papers, a JOSS/Ink & Switch hybrid is cheap and gives real citability. Keep the canonical HTML page. Tag each published version in git. Deposit a frozen snapshot (static HTML, PDF and code) on Zenodo for a free DataCite DOI with ORCID-linked authors. Print the DOI and catalogue number in the paper's footer. That gives a stable citation without a journal.
- Distill's end is a warning against running a peer-reviewed venue on volunteer effort. A house imprint with light editorial review and self-publishing infrastructure is more sustainable at 3die's size.

### Gaps
- Not checked: Open Humanities Press, Mattering Press, Leonardo/MIT Press, and Distill's own licence (background, unverified: CC BY 4.0 for text and figures, code on GitHub).
- Ink & Switch's licence is unknown.
- Did not check whether a non-academic micro-publisher can join Crossref, or what DOAB's listing rules are.

## Catalogue numbers, shared identity and multi-author credit

### Takeaway
Numbered series are normal: Blank Forms anthology vols. 01–10 and art editions, Shelter Press *Spectres* I–III, Urbanomic *Collapse* volumes and the *Redactions*/*Mono* series. Very different texts are held together by a series name plus design (B42's typographic identity), not by subject.

### Cited Findings
- Blank Forms numbers its anthologies (vol. 09, vol. 10) and its art editions ("Art Edition 7") separately. — [Artbook: Blank Forms series](https://Artbook.com/blank-forms-series.html); [blankforms.org](https://www.blankforms.org/publications/graham-lambkin-joe-mcphee-the-wolf-has-come-down-from-the-north-art-edition-7)
- Urbanomic runs several named series (*Collapse*, *Redactions*, *Mono*). Edited volumes credit an editor plus contributors, for example *Hydroplutonic Kernow*, edited by Mackay with a foreword by Caitlin DeSilvey, and *Chronosis* by Negarestani, Tilford and Mackay. — [Penguin Random House: Redactions](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/series/M6L/urbanomic--redactions); [Open Library: Urbanomic](https://openlibrary.org/publishers/Urbanomic)
- B42's books are recognised by their typography and cover experiments, which give a shared look across a design-theory and art list. — [Pen Online](https://pen-online.com/culture/in-france-independent-publishers-are-redefining-the-art-book/)
- Shelter Press's *Spectres* volumes are bilingual (EN/FR) multi-author anthologies. — [Bleep listing](https://bleep.com/merch/256599-various-artists-spectres-iii-ghosts-in-the-machine)
- JOSS ties author lists to the archived software record and encourages ORCID iDs. — [JOSS sample messages](https://github.com/openjournals/joss/blob/main/docs/sample_messages.md)

### Inferences
- A label-style catalogue number such as "3DIE-T001" fits the precedents. Keep it separate from music cat. nos (T for texts, distinct from record prefixes) and print it on the web page, PDF and print colophon. Where the work is printed, also give it an ISBN. In the Czech Republic, ISBNs come free from the national ISBN agency at the National Library (background knowledge, unverified here). In France they come from AFNIL.
- For several authors, the house style can follow JOSS and edited-volume practice: an ordered author list with ORCID, and roles (text, code, figures, editing) in a colophon.
- A shared identity for very different texts can come from one web template (the type, colophon block and cat. no. badge across the site's paper pages), as Distill's template unified its articles.

### Gaps
- No source found on how music labels that publish texts number them against their record catalogue.
- Czech and French ISBN and legal-deposit rules were not verified in this pass.

## Licences for text and code

### Takeaway
OA presses mostly use CC BY-SA (meson) or CC BY-NC-SA (punctum), and both let authors keep copyright under non-exclusive licences. NC licences are disputed as "not really open". Interactive works with code usually split the licence: CC for text and figures, an OSI licence for code (JOSS requires an open-source licence).

### Cited Findings
- punctum uses CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 by default, accepts the author's choice of CC or copyleft licence, and uses a non-exclusive, irrevocable licence with copyright kept by the author. — [punctum submit](https://punctumbooks.com/submit); [punctum contract](https://docs.punctumbooks.com/books/about-our-book-production-process/page/contract)
- meson prefers CC BY-SA, and Bunz criticised NC licences. — [meson OA statement](https://meson.press/we-are-committed-to-open-access/); [Bunz PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/7799415/files/meson%20press%20(M%20Bunz).pdf)
- JOSS review checks that the software carries an OSI-approved open-source licence. — [JOSS review criteria](https://joss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/review_criteria.html)
- An Ink & Switch-affiliated UIST 2024 paper is CC BY 4.0. — [MyWebstrates UIST 2024 PDF](https://cs.au.dk/~clemens/files/MyWebstrates-UIST2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- A sensible default for 3die: text and figures under CC BY 4.0, or CC BY-NC-SA where an author wants to block commercial reuse; code under MIT or MPL-2.0 (GPL only if the author wants copyleft); a non-exclusive licence to 3die, with authors keeping copyright, as at punctum. Authors stay free to republish with a university press or journal later.
- NC licences conflict with selling print editions only if a third party wants to print. The imprint, as licensee, can still sell its own editions.

### Gaps
- Not covered: how CC licences interact with French droit moral or Czech author's-rights law (moral rights cannot be waived in either country), or with EU database rights for datasets in papers.

## Money, print-on-demand, distribution, fairs

### Takeaway
Small art-and-theory presses fund themselves through nonprofit grants (Primary Information: Warhol Foundation), library memberships (Open Book Collective for scholar-led OA presses), and sales of physical copies through specialist distributors (D.A.P., Les presses du réel, Idea Books, Motto, Public Knowledge) and art-book fairs. Small Press Distribution, named in the brief, **closed in March 2024** and is no longer an option. Print on demand lets OA presses (meson) avoid holding stock.

### Cited Findings
- **SPD is defunct.** It closed abruptly in late March 2024 after 55 years, leaving more than 350 presses without distribution and some unpaid. It had run a GoFundMe in February 2023 and lost about $125k in donations that year. — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/94690-small-press-distribution-shuts-down.html); [Wikipedia: SPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Press_Distribution); [CLMP dissolution overview](https://www.clmp.org/about-the-closure-of-small-press-distribution-faqs/overview-of-the-spd-dissolution-process/); [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/the-small-press-world-is-about-to-fall-apart-on-the-collapse-of-small-press-distribution)
- Specialist art-book distributors in use: D.A.P./Artbook (Blank Forms, Set Margins'), Les presses du réel (Shelter Press, Blank Forms in Europe), Idea Books, Public Knowledge and Perimeter (Set Margins'), Motto (Urbanomic), MIT Press via PRH (Strange Attractor, Urbanomic, Semiotext(e), Sternberg). — [Artbook: Set Margins'](https://www.artbook.com/set-margins.html); [Les presses du réel](https://www.lespressesdureel.com/EN/editeur.php?id=155); [Motto](https://www.mottodistribution.com/shop/publishers/urbanomic.html); [Urbanomic distribution](https://www.urbanomic.com/announcement-new-distribution/)
- **Bookvault** is a UK print-on-demand service (Printondemand-worldwide / CopyTECH, Peterborough). It prints mainly for the UK and Europe, offers Shopify integration and a retail network, and its own UK storefront is the Great British Bookshop. Indie guides pair it with IngramSpark for trade and library distribution and KDP for Amazon. These are vendor-adjacent sources. — [ScribeCount on Bookvault](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/publishing-wide/bookvault); [European Purpose: Bookvault](https://europeanpurpose.com/tool/bookvault); [Bookvault: sell worldwide](https://bookvault.app/sell-books-worldwide/); [IngramSpark UK](https://www.ingramspark.com/blog/the-united-kingdom-opportunity-with-ingramspark)
- meson prints on demand and gives digital editions free. — [meson about](https://meson.press/about/)
- **Fairs:** The GHMP Art Book Fair (Prague, Troja Château) is the main Prague art-book fair. Its fifth edition ran 20–21 Sept 2025, entry is free, and previous editions had more than 100 Czech and international exhibitors. The 2026 edition was set for 19–20 Sept 2026, and its call was open to publishers, art schools and fanzine makers. FaVU VUT Brno exhibited its publishing there in 2025. — [GHMP exhibitors 2026](https://www.ghmp.cz/en/exhibitors-at-the-2026-art-book-fair/); [GHMP Art Book Fair 26](https://www.ghmp.cz/en/accompanying-events/ghmp-art-book-fair-26/); [FaVU VUT at GHMP ABF 2025](https://www.favu.vut.cz/en/students/news-and-calls/f26745/d302357); [Prague.eu](https://prague.eu/en/akce/art-book-fair/)
- **Offprint Paris** 2026 ran 22–25 Oct at Césure, and the 2025 edition had more than 150 independent publishers. **Miss Read** Berlin 2026 ran 26–28 June at silent green, its 18th edition, themed on bibliodiversity. B42 exhibits at the Paris Ass Book Fair. — [offprint.org](https://offprint.org); [e-flux: Offprint Paris](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/6788412/offprint-paris); [e-flux: Miss Read 2026](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/6787045/miss-read-2026); [Paris Ass Book Fair: B42](https://parisassbookfair.fr/en/exposants/editions-b42/)
- Primary Information pays artists and staff fairly (W.A.G.E. certified) and has foundation support. — [A.R.T. conversation](https://www.artresourcestransfer.org/resources/notes/primary-information-in-conversation-with-a-r-t); [Warhol Foundation](https://warholfoundation.org/grants/archive/primary-information)

### Inferences
- A low-effort path for 3die: free web plus a Zenodo snapshot, print on demand (Bookvault for the UK/EU, or IngramSpark for trade reach) for a perfect-bound edition of the longer papers, and small runs (risograph or digital) for fair stock. Start at GHMP ABF Prague, which is local, free for visitors and open to small and fanzine makers, then Offprint, Paris Ass Book Fair or Miss Read. A specialist distributor (Les presses du réel, Motto, Idea Books) becomes worth approaching after a few titles exist.
- The money will most likely come from sales of physical editions at fairs and in the 3die shop, plus grants. Czech grants such as the Ministry of Culture literature and publishing calls and the Státní fond kultury were not verified here. Membership or donation models (Open Book Collective, Patreon-style) suit only once there is a list.

### Gaps
- No pricing found for Lulu, Blurb, KDP, Bookvault or Czech/EU printers (e.g. Finidr, Tiskárna Helbich, Graspo), nor risograph studio rates in Prague or Paris.
- Not checked: Printed Matter's NY/LA Art Book Fair, Antenne Books' current status, or Idea Books' terms for new publishers.
- No verified data on Czech VAT on books, legal-deposit copy obligations, or French dépôt légal for a France-registered imprint.
