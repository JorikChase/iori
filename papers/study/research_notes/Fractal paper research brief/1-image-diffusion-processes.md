# What noise and what forward/reverse process today's image diffusion models actually use

Research notes, 2026-10-11. Scope: the forward (noising) process, the noise schedule, the space it runs in, and the reverse (sampling) computation for each major image-diffusion family, plus the three classical results the paper leans on (Anderson 1982, Tweedie/Miyasawa/Efron, Vincent 2011). Primary sources were used wherever the text could be fetched; where a paper leaves a number out (e.g. SDXL never prints its β schedule) the number is taken from the released configuration files and marked as such. Everything older than 2024 is explicitly dated.

Convention used throughout: `w`/`W` is a standard Wiener process (Brownian motion); "OU" means an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (Brownian motion with a linear restoring drift); "VP" = variance preserving, "VE" = variance exploding.

---

## Key question 1 — For each family: is the forward process Brownian/Wiener (with or without drift)? What is the SDE or the discrete update? Is the path deterministic (flow matching) while the per-level noise is still Gaussian?

### Takeaway
DDPM / Stable Diffusion 1.x–2.x / SDXL run a *discretised Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (VP-SDE)* forward process — Brownian motion with a linear drift toward zero, so the variance stays ≈1; NCSN/VE models run *driftless, time-changed Brownian motion*. SD3, FLUX.1/FLUX.2 (rectified flow / flow matching) replace the SDE with a *deterministic straight-line interpolation* `z_t = (1−t)·x0 + t·ε` between data and a single Gaussian draw; every intermediate marginal is still "data + Gaussian noise", but the path itself is not a Wiener path.

### Cited Findings

**DDPM (Ho, Jain, Abbeel, 2020 — pre-2021, foundational)**
- Forward step: `q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = N(x_t; sqrt(1−β_t) x_{t-1}, β_t I)`, with `α_t := 1−β_t`, `ᾱ_t := ∏_{s≤t} α_s`; closed form `q(x_t | x_0) = N(x_t; sqrt(ᾱ_t) x_0, (1−ᾱ_t) I)` (Eqs. 2 and 4) — [Ho et al. 2020, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)
- The latents `x_1…x_T` have the same dimensionality as the data (image-shaped tensors; CIFAR-10 32×32×3, CelebA-HQ/LSUN 256×256×3, pixels scaled to [−1, 1]) — [Ho et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)
- The paper's own framing: the ε-parameterisation "reveals an equivalence with denoising score matching over multiple noise levels during training" and with annealed Langevin dynamics during sampling — [Ho et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)

**Score-SDE framing (Song, Sohl-Dickstein, Kingma, Kumar, Ermon, Poole, ICLR 2021 — pre-2022, foundational)**
- General forward SDE: `dx = f(x,t) dt + g(t) dw`, "where w is a standard Wiener process" (Eq. 5) — [Song et al. 2021, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- VE-SDE (Eq. 9): `dx = sqrt(d[σ²(t)]/dt) dw` — no drift; this is Brownian motion run on the clock `σ²(t)` — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- VP-SDE (Eq. 11): `dx = −½ β(t) x dt + sqrt(β(t)) dw` — Brownian motion with a linear drift, i.e. a (time-inhomogeneous) Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- sub-VP SDE (Eq. 12): `dx = −½ β(t) x dt + sqrt(β(t)(1 − e^{−2∫₀ᵗ β}))) dw` — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- The DDPM noise chain `x_i = sqrt(1−β_i) x_{i−1} + sqrt(β_i) z_{i−1}` (Eq. 10) converges to the VP-SDE; the SMLD/NCSN chain `x_i = x_{i−1} + sqrt(σ_i² − σ_{i−1}²) z_{i−1}` (Eq. 8) converges to the VE-SDE — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- The VP process "yields a process with a fixed variance of one when the initial distribution has unit variance"; VE "has exploding variance as t→∞"; the SMLD terminal marginal is `p_{σmax}(x) ≈ N(0, σ_max² I)` — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)

**Stable Diffusion 1.x / 2.x (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022, "latent diffusion" — pre-2023)**
- LDM objective: `L_LDM = E[ ‖ε − ε_θ(z_t, t)‖²₂ ]` with `z = E(x)`, `ε ~ N(0, I)`, "t uniformly sampled from {1,…,T}"; "Since the forward process is fixed, z_t can be efficiently obtained from E during training"; diffusion is described as "learning the reverse process of a fixed Markov Chain of length T" — the same DDPM chain, applied to `z` instead of pixels — [Rombach et al. 2022, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10752)
- The released SD v1 config fixes `timesteps: 1000`, `linear_start: 0.00085`, `linear_end: 0.0120` — [CompVis v1-inference.yaml](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/configs/stable-diffusion/v1-inference.yaml)
- Same chain in Diffusers' SD 1.5 scheduler: `beta_schedule: "scaled_linear"`, `beta_start: 0.00085`, `beta_end: 0.012`, `num_train_timesteps: 1000` — [SD 1.5 scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)

**SDXL (Podell et al., 2023)**
- "a discrete-time diffusion schedule" with 1000 steps, citing Ho et al. 2020; the noise distribution `p(σ)` is "a discrete distribution over 1000 noise levels"; the model "requires offset-noise for aesthetically pleasing results", offset-noise level 0.05 in the final stage — [Podell et al. 2023, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952)
- Released SDXL config: `DiscreteDenoiser`, `num_idx: 1000`, `LegacyDDPMDiscretization`, `EpsScaling` (ε-prediction) — [sd_xl_base.yaml](https://github.com/Stability-AI/generative-models/blob/main/configs/inference/sd_xl_base.yaml); Diffusers: `EulerDiscreteScheduler`, `scaled_linear`, `beta_start 0.00085`, `beta_end 0.012`, `prediction_type: "epsilon"`, `timestep_spacing: "leading"` — [SDXL scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)
- SDXL's Appendix C re-expresses the model in the continuous (Song 2021 / Karras 2022) framework with `s_θ = (D_θ − x)/σ²`, but states the trained model itself uses the discrete formulation; "The EDM-framework of Karras et al. 2022 is a promising candidate for future model training" — [Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952)

**Rectified Flow (Liu, Gong, Liu, 2022/ICLR 2023) and Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022/ICLR 2023)**
- Rectified flow: for `X_0 ~ π_0`, `X_1 ~ π_1` the linear interpolation `X_t = t·X_1 + (1−t)·X_0`; the rectified flow is the ODE `dZ_t = v(Z_t, t) dt`, `Z_0 ~ π_0`, with `v` fit by least squares `min_v ∫₀¹ E‖(X_1 − X_0) − v(X_t, t)‖² dt`; the paper notes the interpolation `X_t` alone is "non-causal" (needs `X_1`) and fitting `v` "causalizes" it — [Liu et al. 2022, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03003)
- For image generation `π_0` is standard Gaussian noise (Figure 1 caption) — [Liu et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03003)
- "All variants of PF-ODEs can be viewed as instances of" the nonlinear framework `X_t = α_t X_1 + β_t ξ`, `ξ ~ N(0,I)`; PF-ODE paths are "generally curved" and cannot be straightened by reflow; linear interpolation is recommended as the default — [Liu et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03003)
- Flow matching: `L_FM = E_{t,p_t(x)} ‖v_t(x) − u_t(x)‖²`; conditional form `L_CFM = E_{t,q(x_1),p_t(x|x_1)} ‖v_t(x) − u_t(x|x_1)‖²` with identical gradients (Theorem 2) — [Lipman et al. 2022, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)
- Gaussian conditional path `p_t(x|x_1) = N(x | μ_t(x_1), σ_t(x_1)² I)`; the generating field `u_t(x|x_1) = (σ'_t/σ_t)(x − μ_t) + μ'_t` (Theorem 3); base `p_0 = N(0, I)`; "Each conditional path is Gaussian at every t"; the marginal path is a mixture and "is therefore not Gaussian in general" — [Lipman et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)
- The VE and VP diffusion paths are special cases: VE `p_t(x|x_1) = N(x | x_1, σ²_{1−t} I)`, VP `p_t(x|x_1) = N(x | α_{1−t} x_1, (1−α²_{1−t}) I)` with `α_t = e^{−½T(t)}`, `T(t) = ∫₀ᵗ β`; the OT path `μ_t = t x_1`, `σ_t = 1 − (1−σ_min) t` gives `u_t(x|x_1) = (x_1 − (1−σ_min) x)/(1 − (1−σ_min) t)`, under which particles "always move in straight line trajectories and with constant speed" — [Lipman et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)

**SD3 (Esser et al., 2024 — "Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers")**
- Forward process (Eq. 13): `z_t = (1 − t)·x_0 + t·ε`, `ε ~ N(0, I)`; "We always apply the forward process from Equation 2 in the latent space" (App. B.2) — [Esser et al. 2024, arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)
- Objective: `L_CFM = E ‖v_Θ(z,t) − u_t(z|ε)‖²`; the paper rewrites it as a *weighted noise-prediction loss* `L_w = −½ E[w_t λ'_t ‖ε_Θ(z_t,t) − ε‖²]` (Eq. 12), with `w_t^{RF} = t/(1−t)` for rectified flow, and notes "the optimum of the above objective does not change when introducing a time-dependent weighting" — i.e. rectified flow is formally a diffusion model with a particular schedule and loss weight — [Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)
- The reference implementation's noising is literally `sigma * noise + (1.0 − sigma) * latent_image` and the denoised estimate `model_input − model_output * sigma` — [Stability-AI/sd3.5 sd3_impls.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_impls.py)

**FLUX.1 (Black Forest Labs, Aug 2024) and FLUX.2 (Nov 2025)**
- Official launch post (2024-08-01): "All public FLUX.1 models are based on a hybrid architecture of multimodal and parallel diffusion transformer blocks", "scaled to 12B parameters", "building on flow matching, a general and conceptually simple method for training generative models, which includes diffusion as a special case"; FLUX.1 [dev] is "guidance-distilled" from [pro]; the post promised a tech report that, as far as I could find, was never published — [bfl.ai blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/24-08-01-bfl)
- Hugging Face model card: "FLUX.1 [dev] is a 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer" — [FLUX.1-dev model card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev)
- Official inference code: initial noise is `torch.randn(...)` (`get_noise`); the denoising loop is `img = img + (t_prev − t_curr) * pred` — a plain Euler step on the rectified-flow ODE with `t=1` pure noise — [black-forest-labs/flux sampling.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/sampling.py)
- Diffusers' scheduler for SD3/FLUX (`FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler`) implements the forward process as `sample = sigma * noise + (1.0 − sigma) * sample` and the step as `prev_sample = sample + dt * model_output` with `dt = sigma_next − sigma`; optional `stochastic_sampling` re-noises `x0` with fresh Gaussian noise each step — [diffusers scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py)
- FLUX.2 (released 2025-11-25) is described by secondary sources as a latent rectified-flow transformer paired with a ~24B Mistral-3 vision-language encoder, with a retrained VAE; BFL "has not published full architectural details" — [SiliconANGLE 2025-12-01](https://siliconangle.com/2025/12/01/open-source-image-generator-startup-black-forest-labs-raises-300m/); [Prodia FLUX.2 docs](https://docs.prodia.com/models/flux-2/) (both secondary; see Gaps)

**EDM (Karras, Aittala, Aila, Laine, NeurIPS 2022) — the σ-parametrisation**
- Setup: "the family of mollified distributions p(x; σ) obtained by adding i.i.d. Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ to the data"; sampling starts at `x_0 ~ N(0, σ_max² I)` and walks `σ_0 = σ_max > σ_1 > … > σ_N = 0` — [Karras et al. 2022, arXiv PDF §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- Probability-flow ODE (Eq. 1): `dx = −σ̇(t) σ(t) ∇_x log p(x; σ(t)) dt`; general form with scaling `s(t)` (Eq. 4); the paper argues "the best choice for these functions is σ(t) = t and s(t) = 1" (which is also DDIM's choice) — [Karras et al. 2022 §2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- Their stochastic generalisation (Eq. 6): `dx± = −σ̇σ ∇log p dt ± β(t)σ(t)² ∇log p dt + sqrt(2β(t)) σ(t) dω_t`, "where ω_t is the standard Wiener process"; "The SDEs of Song et al. are recovered with the choice β(t) = σ̇(t)/σ(t), whereby the score vanishes from the forward SDE"; the forward and backward SDEs are "related by the time reversal formula of Anderson" — [Karras et al. 2022 §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- Note the EDM paper itself does not use a forward SDE at all: training corrupts with `n = randn_like(y) * sigma` directly (one Gaussian draw per sample), sigma drawn per-sample — [NVlabs/edm training/loss.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/training/loss.py)

**Consistency models (Song, Dhariwal, Chen, Sutskever, ICML 2023) — briefly**
- A consistency model maps any point of a PF-ODE trajectory to its origin: `f: (x_t, t) ↦ x_ε`, with `f(x_t,t) = f(x_{t'},t')` on the same trajectory and boundary condition `f(x_ε, ε) = x_ε`; built on the EDM PF-ODE `dx_t/dt = −t·s_φ(x_t,t)` with `T = 80`, `ε = 0.002`, pixels in [−1, 1] — [Song et al. 2023, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)
- So distillation does not change the forward process: the noising is still `x + t·z`, `z ~ N(0,I)` (consistency training even replaces the score by the unbiased estimator `−(x_t − x)/t²`) — [Song et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)

### Inferences
- DDPM / SD1–2 / SDXL: the per-step update `x_t = sqrt(1−β_t) x_{t−1} + sqrt(β_t) z` is exactly an Euler–Maruyama step of the VP-SDE (Song et al. show the limit). So "Brownian with drift" is the right description: Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, variance-preserving. It is *not* a pure Wiener process (there is a restoring drift `−½βx`), and strictly it is a Markov chain of 1000 Gaussian kernels, not a continuous path.
- VE/NCSN and EDM-trained models: driftless. `x(t) = x(0) + W_{σ²(t)}` is Brownian motion with a deterministic time change. This is the one family where "the noise is literally Brownian motion" needs no qualifier (up to the time change).
- SD3 / FLUX: for a fixed pair `(x_0, ε)` the forward path is a straight line, so it is *not* a Wiener path and has no Brownian increments. However, `z_t | x_0 ~ N((1−t)x_0, t² I)`, so every noise level is additive Gaussian noise with std `t/(1−t)` relative to the signal. Because this is a Gaussian path of the form `a_t x_0 + b_t ε` (a = 1−t, b = t), Song et al.'s general VP form implies there exists an Itô SDE with the same marginals, `dx = −x/(1−t) dt + sqrt(2t/(1−t)) dw` (one checks `2bḃ − 2(ȧ/a)b² = 2t/(1−t) ≥ 0`). This is my derivation, not a quoted result; it is consistent with the SD3 paper's explicit statement that rectified flow is a diffusion objective with weight `t/(1−t)` and with Liu et al.'s statement that PF-ODEs are instances of `α_t X_1 + β_t ξ`. The honest wording is: the *marginals* are those of a Brownian (OU-type) diffusion; the *path used in training and sampling* is a deterministic interpolation.
- All distillation families (consistency, LCM, guidance-distilled FLUX.1 [dev]/[schnell]) inherit their teacher's forward process; they only change how the reverse map is computed.

## Key question 2 — What exact noise schedule does each use (linear β, cosine, EDM log-normal σ, logit-normal t)? Numbers.

### Takeaway
DDPM: linear β from 1e−4 to 0.02 over T=1000. Stable Diffusion 1.x/2.x/SDXL: the "scaled linear" schedule — β linear in *square-root space* from 0.00085 to 0.012 over 1000 steps (never printed in the papers; it is in the configs). EDM: continuous σ ∈ [0.002, 80] with training σ drawn log-normally, ln σ ~ N(−1.2, 1.2²), and ρ=7 sampling spacing. SD3: t ∈ [0,1] drawn logit-normal (m=0, s=1) with a resolution shift α=3 at 1024²; FLUX: same interpolation with an exponential time shift whose μ is a linear function of the token count (0.5 at 256 tokens → 1.15 at 4096).

### Cited Findings
- DDPM: `T = 1000`, β "increasing linearly from β1 = 10⁻⁴ to βT = 0.02" — [Ho et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)
- Score-SDE continuous VP: `β(t) = β̄_min + t(β̄_max − β̄_min)`, t ∈ [0,1], with `β̄_min = 0.1`, `β̄_max = 20` ("matching Ho et al."); VE: `σ(t) = σ_min (σ_max/σ_min)^t`, `σ_min = 0.01`, σ_max chosen per Song & Ermon 2020 "Technique 1" — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- EDM's reference reimplementation of those baselines: VP `β_d = 19.9`, `β_min = 0.1`, `ε_t = 1e−5`, `σ(t) = sqrt(exp(½β_d t² + β_min t) − 1)`, training `t` uniform; VE `σ_min = 0.02`, `σ_max = 100`, `ln σ` uniform — [NVlabs/edm loss.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/training/loss.py); EDM Table 1 lists the same (VP: `β_d = 19.9, β_min = 0.1, ε_s = 10⁻³, ε_t = 10⁻⁵, M = 1000`; VE: `σ_min = 0.02, σ_max = 100`; iDDPM: `ᾱ_j = sin²(π/2 · j/(M(C_2+1)))`, `C_1 = 0.001, C_2 = 0.008, M = 1000, j_0 = 8`) — [Karras et al. 2022 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- Stable Diffusion 1.x: `linear_start: 0.00085`, `linear_end: 0.0120`, `timesteps: 1000` — [CompVis v1-inference.yaml](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/configs/stable-diffusion/v1-inference.yaml); and the "linear" schedule in that codebase is actually `betas = linspace(linear_start**0.5, linear_end**0.5, n)**2` — [CompVis ldm/modules/diffusionmodules/util.py `make_beta_schedule`](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/ldm/modules/diffusionmodules/util.py); Diffusers names this `"scaled_linear"` — [SD 1.5 scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)
- SDXL: identical β range (`beta_start 0.00085`, `beta_end 0.012`, `scaled_linear`, 1000 steps, ε-prediction) — [SDXL scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json); `LegacyDDPMDiscretization`, `num_idx: 1000` — [sd_xl_base.yaml](https://github.com/Stability-AI/generative-models/blob/main/configs/inference/sd_xl_base.yaml); plus offset noise 0.05; refiner trained on "the first 200 discrete noise scales" — [Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952)
- EDM training: `ln(σ) ~ N(P_mean, P_std²)` with `P_mean = −1.2`, `P_std = 1.2`, `σ_data = 0.5`; loss weight `λ(σ) = (σ² + σ_data²)/(σ σ_data)²` — [Karras et al. 2022 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364); code: `sigma = (rnd_normal * P_std + P_mean).exp()` — [NVlabs/edm loss.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/training/loss.py)
- EDM sampling: `σ_i = (σ_max^{1/ρ} + i/(N−1)(σ_min^{1/ρ} − σ_max^{1/ρ}))^ρ`, `σ_min = 0.002`, `σ_max = 80`, `ρ = 7` ("We set ρ = 7 for the remainder of this paper"), default `num_steps = 18` — [Karras et al. 2022 §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364); [NVlabs/edm generate.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/generate.py)
- EDM preconditioning: `c_skip = σ_data²/(σ² + σ_data²)`, `c_out = σ σ_data / sqrt(σ² + σ_data²)`, `c_in = 1/sqrt(σ_data² + σ²)`, `c_noise = ln(σ)/4` — [NVlabs/edm networks.py EDMPrecond](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/training/networks.py)
- Flow matching (Lipman): `t ~ U[0,1]`, OT path `σ_t = 1 − (1−σ_min) t` — [Lipman et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)
- SD3 timestep density: logit-normal `π_ln(t; m, s) = 1/(s sqrt(2π)) · 1/(t(1−t)) · exp(−(logit(t) − m)²/(2s²))`; the comparison (Table 1) also tried mode sampling `f_mode(u; s) = 1 − u − s(cos²(πu/2) − 1 + u)` and CosMap `t = 1 − 1/(tan(πu/2) + 1)`; winner `rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00)` (m = 0, s = 1), average rank 1.54 — [Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)
- SD3 resolution shift (Eq. 23): `t_m = sqrt(m/n)·t_n / (1 + (sqrt(m/n) − 1) t_n)` with `α = sqrt(m/n)`; `α = 3.0` used "during both training and sampling" at 1024×1024; equivalently `λ_{t_m} = λ_{t_n} − 2 log α` in log-SNR — [Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206)
- SD3/SD3.5 reference code: `sigma(t) = shift·t/(1 + (shift − 1)·t)`, `shift` default 1.0, "publicly released models use 3.0" (`SHIFT = 3.0`), 1000 discrete timesteps — [Stability-AI/sd3.5 sd3_impls.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_impls.py), [sd3_infer.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_infer.py)
- FLUX.1 schedule: `time_shift(mu, sigma, t) = exp(mu)/(exp(mu) + (1/t − 1)^sigma)` with `mu = get_lin_function(x1=256, y1=0.5, x2=4096, y2=1.15)(image_seq_len)` (so μ ≈ 0.5 at 256 latent tokens and 1.15 at 4096 tokens, i.e. 1024² px after 2×2 patching) — [black-forest-labs/flux sampling.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/sampling.py); Diffusers reproduces this as `calculate_shift(image_seq_len, 256, 4096, 0.5, 1.15)` and `_time_shift_exponential` — [diffusers pipeline_flux.py](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/pipelines/flux/pipeline_flux.py), [scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py)

### Inferences
- The SD1/2/SDXL "scaled linear" schedule reaches `ᾱ_T ≈ 0.0047` (not zero), so the terminal state is not exactly pure noise — this is the known "non-zero terminal SNR" issue that SDXL's Appendix C alludes to when it says the EDM framework "does not require noise-schedule corrections". (Computed from the config numbers; not stated in the papers.)
- In EDM units the SD3 interpolation `z_t = (1−t)x_0 + tε` has `σ_EDM = t/(1−t)`; the shift `α=3` maps `t = 0.5` (σ=1) to `t' = 0.75` (σ=3), i.e. it pushes training toward higher noise for larger images. The logit-normal `m=0, s=1` is symmetric in log-SNR around σ_EDM = 1 — the same centring idea as EDM's `P_mean = −1.2` (σ ≈ 0.3 on σ_data = 0.5 data), just a different centre.
- In Lipman's notation `σ_min` is a tiny floor (not quoted numerically in the fetched text); SD3/FLUX drop it (`t` reaches 0 exactly: `sigs += [0.0]`).

- Neither the LDM paper nor the SDXL paper prints β_start/β_end; the numbers above are from the released configs (primary, but not the papers).
- The SD3 paper's Appendix B.3 text fetched did not state the final 8B model's training-time `m, s` or the exact shift at inference; the reference code (`SHIFT = 3.0`) is used for the latter.
- Lipman's `σ_min` value was not in the portion of text I could read.

## Key question 3 — In what space (pixel, 4- or 16-channel VAE latent, resolution)?

### Takeaway
DDPM, Score-SDE, EDM, consistency models: pixels (32²–256², EDM up to ImageNet-64). SD 1.x/2.x/SDXL: a KL-regularised VAE latent with f=8 spatial downsampling and 4 channels (64×64×4 for 512², 128×128×4 for SDXL 1024²). SD3 and FLUX.1: f=8, 16-channel latent (128×128×16 at 1024²); FLUX further packs 2×2 latent patches into 64-channel tokens.

### Cited Findings
- DDPM: "the 32×32×3 or 256×256×3 images"; latents same shape as data — [Ho et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)
- Consistency models: pixels rescaled to [−1, 1] — [Song et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)
- EDM: record FIDs on CIFAR-10 and "ImageNet in 64×64 resolution", pixel space, `σ_data = 0.5` — [Karras et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- LDM: encoder "downsamples the image by a factor f = H/h = W/w", f = 2^m tested; "KL-reg. imposes a slight KL-penalty towards a standard normal on the learned latent"; the text-to-image model is "LDM-KL-8"; main experiments at 256×256 (→ 32×32 latent) — [Rombach et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10752). The channel count is not in the fetched text; the released SD v1 config fixes `channels: 4`, `embed_dim: 4`, `z_channels: 4`, `image_size: 64` (latent, for 512² px), `scale_factor: 0.18215` — [CompVis v1-inference.yaml](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/configs/stable-diffusion/v1-inference.yaml); `AutoencoderKL`, `latent_channels: 4`, `block_out_channels [128,256,512,512]` (three 2× downsamples → f=8), `sample_size: 512` — [SD 1.5 vae/config.json](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/blob/main/vae/config.json)
- SDXL: autoencoder "trained from scratch" with the SD architecture (batch 256 vs 9, EMA); 1024² px ↔ 128×128 latent; multi-aspect buckets near 1024² in multiples of 64; the refiner is "in the same latent space" — [Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952); `latent_channels: 4`, `scaling_factor: 0.13025`, `sample_size: 1024` — [SDXL vae/config.json](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/blob/main/vae/config.json); `z_channels: 4`, `embed_dim: 4`, UNet `in_channels: 4` — [sd_xl_base.yaml](https://github.com/Stability-AI/generative-models/blob/main/configs/inference/sd_xl_base.yaml)
- SD3: "d = 16 latent channels" with f = 8 for all models; "increasing the number of latent channels d significantly boosts reconstruction performance" (4/8/16 ch: rFID 2.41/1.56/1.06, PSNR 25.12/26.40/28.62); "We opt for the 16-chn model as we ultimately aim to scale to much larger model sizes" — [Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206); reference code allocates latents of shape `(batch, 16, height//8, width//8)` — [sd3_infer.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_infer.py)
- FLUX.1: autoencoder `ch_mult = [1, 2, 4, 4]` (f = 8), `z_channels = 16`, `scale_factor = 0.3611`, `shift_factor = 0.1159`; transformer `in_channels = 64` (2×2 patches of 16-ch latent) — [black-forest-labs/flux util.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/util.py)
- FLUX.2: "retrained from scratch" VAE, native output up to 4 MP — secondary sources only ([Prodia docs](https://docs.prodia.com/models/flux-2/)); latent channel count not verified.

### Inferences
- The 2022→2024 shift from 4 to 16 latent channels (SD3, FLUX) is driven by reconstruction fidelity; it does not change the nature of the noise, which is still i.i.d. Gaussian per latent element.
- Because SD-family noise is added in a *learned, compressed* space, "noise in Stable Diffusion" is Gaussian in latent coordinates and only approximately so in pixel coordinates (through a nonlinear decoder). This matters for any claim that compares the noise to a physical Brownian field in the image plane.

- SD 2.x 768-v latent is presumably 96×96×4 (f=8); not verified this session (see Q1 gaps).

## Key question 4 — How is the reverse process solved (ancestral sampling, DDIM, Euler, Heun, ODE)?

### Takeaway
DDPM: stochastic ancestral sampling (1000 steps), shown by Song et al. to be a discretisation of Anderson's reverse-time SDE. SD 1.x–SDXL: in practice deterministic ODE solvers on the same model — DDIM (η=0) in the papers, PNDM/Euler/DPM-Solver++ in the shipped pipelines — 20–50 steps. EDM: Heun 2nd-order on the σ(t)=t PF-ODE, 18–35 NFE, optional "churn". SD3/FLUX: Euler on the rectified-flow ODE (SD3.5 reference defaults to DPM-Solver++(2M), 40 steps); consistency/distilled models: one to a few evaluations.

### Cited Findings
- DDPM Algorithm 2: from `x_T ~ N(0, I)`, for t = T…1: `x_{t−1} = (1/sqrt(α_t)) (x_t − ((1−α_t)/sqrt(1−ᾱ_t)) ε_θ(x_t, t)) + σ_t z`, `z ~ N(0,I)` (z = 0 at t = 1); `σ_t² = β_t` and `σ_t² = β̃_t = ((1−ᾱ_{t−1})/(1−ᾱ_t)) β_t` "had similar results" — [Ho et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.11239)
- Reverse-time SDE (Song Eq. 6): `dx = [f(x,t) − g(t)² ∇_x log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) dw̄`, w̄ a reverse-time Wiener process, attributed to Anderson (1982): "A remarkable result from Anderson 1982 states that the reverse of a diffusion process is also a diffusion process"; DDPM ancestral sampling "actually corresponds to one special discretization of the reverse-time VP SDE"; Predictor–Corrector samplers pair an SDE solver with score-based MCMC — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- Probability-flow ODE (Song Eq. 13): `dx = [f(x,t) − ½ g(t)² ∇_x log p_t(x)] dt`, sharing the SDE's marginals `p_t` — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- DDIM (Song, Meng, Ermon 2020) update (Eq. 12): `x_{t−1} = sqrt(α_{t−1}) ((x_t − sqrt(1−α_t) ε_θ)/sqrt(α_t)) + sqrt(1 − α_{t−1} − σ_t²) ε_θ + σ_t ε_t`; with `σ(η)` (Eq. 16) "an original DDPM generative process when η = 1 and DDIM when η = 0"; the η = 0 update "can be treated as a Euler method over the following ODE" `dx̄(t) = ε_θ(x̄/sqrt(σ²+1)) dσ(t)`; 10–100 steps — [Song, Meng, Ermon 2020, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.02502)
- LDM: DDIM throughout (200 steps η=1.0 in Fig. 5; 250 steps Table 2; sweeps over 10–200 steps) — [Rombach et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.10752); shipped SD 1.5 pipeline default `PNDMScheduler` (`skip_prk_steps: true`, `steps_offset: 1`) — [SD 1.5 scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)
- SDXL: "50 steps of the DDIM sampler", cfg 8.0 in the figures; refiner applied SDEdit-style (noise the base latent, denoise with the refiner) — [Podell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.01952); shipped default `EulerDiscreteScheduler` (ε-prediction, "leading" spacing) — [SDXL scheduler_config.json](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/blob/main/scheduler/scheduler_config.json)
- EDM deterministic sampler: Algorithm 1 "Deterministic sampling using Heun's 2nd order method with arbitrary σ(t) and s(t)"; code: `d = (x − D(x;σ))/σ`, Euler step `x_next = x + (σ_next − σ) d`, then 2nd-order correction `x_next = x + (σ_next − σ)(½ d + ½ d')`; stochastic variant (Algorithm 2) adds `S_churn` noise `x̂ = x + sqrt(σ̂² − σ²) S_noise · z` before each step — [Karras et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364); [NVlabs/edm generate.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/edm/blob/main/generate.py)
- Flow matching: integrate the learned ODE from `x_0 ~ N(0,I)` over t ∈ [0,1]; dopri5 at tolerance 1e−5 for evaluation (≈122–142 NFE); midpoint for fixed-step studies — [Lipman et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02747)
- Rectified flow: "Euler method with step size 1/N for N steps"; the 2-rectified flow (after one reflow) "yields good results with one Euler step"; "Perfectly straight paths can be simulated exactly with a single Euler step" — [Liu et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.03003)
- SD3: ODE `dy_t = v_Θ(y_t, t) dt` (Eq. 1); "we always use a Euler discretization"; evaluations at 5/10/25/50 steps — [Esser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03206); SD3.5 reference defaults `SAMPLER = "dpmpp_2m"`, `STEPS = 40` ("sd3_medium is quite decent at 28 steps"), `CFG_SCALE = 4.5`, `SHIFT = 3.0`, with `sample_euler` also provided ("Implements Algorithm 2 (Euler steps) from Karras et al. (2022)") — [sd3_infer.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_infer.py), [sd3_impls.py](https://github.com/Stability-AI/sd3.5/blob/main/sd3_impls.py)
- FLUX.1: `img = img + (t_prev − t_curr) * pred` (Euler) — [flux sampling.py](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/sampling.py); Diffusers `FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler`, `prev_sample = sample + dt * model_output`, optional `stochastic_sampling` — [diffusers](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py)
- Consistency models: one step `x̂_ε = f_θ(x̂_T, T)` with `x̂_T ~ N(0, T² I)`; multistep alternates `x̂_{τ_n} = x + sqrt(τ_n² − ε²) z` and `x = f_θ(x̂_{τ_n}, τ_n)`; trained by distillation from a teacher's ODE step (CD) or without a teacher (CT) — [Song et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)

### Inferences
- The reverse computation in every 2024–2026 production model (SDXL pipelines, SD3, FLUX) is by default *deterministic*: an ODE integration whose only randomness is the initial Gaussian draw (plus optional, off-by-default noise re-injection). The Brownian reverse SDE of Anderson 1982 is the theoretical object; it is rarely the sampler actually run.
- EDM's Eq. 6 is the cleanest statement of the relationship: ODE + Langevin term with rate β(t); Song's SDEs are the special case β = σ̇/σ; ODE samplers are β = 0.

### Gaps
- Exact default step counts / samplers used to produce the SD3 paper figures (Appendix B.3) were cut off in the fetched text; the reference-implementation defaults are used instead.
- FLUX.1 [pro] (API) sampler and step count are not public.

## Key question 5 — Tweedie's formula: state it exactly and cite. Also Anderson 1982, Miyasawa 1961, Vincent 2011.

### Takeaway
Efron (2011): for `μ ~ g(·)`, `z | μ ~ N(μ, σ²)`, `E{μ|z} = z + σ² l'(z)` with `l(z) = log f(z)` the log marginal density (Eq. 1.4), attributed by Robbins (1956) to M. K. Tweedie; posterior variance `σ²(1 + σ² l''(z))` (Eq. 2.8). In diffusion units with `x = x_0 + σ ε` this is exactly EDM's `∇_x log p(x;σ) = (D(x;σ) − x)/σ²`: the optimal denoiser *is* a score step. Anderson 1982 supplies the reverse-time SDE; Vincent 2011 supplies the denoising-score-matching objective that lets a network learn the score from noisy samples alone.

### Cited Findings
- Model (Efron Eq. 1.2): `μ ~ g(·)` and `z|μ ~ N(μ, σ²)`, σ² known; marginal `f(z) = ∫ φ_σ(z − μ) g(μ) dμ` (Eq. 1.3) — [Efron 2011, "Tweedie's Formula and Selection Bias"](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)
- Tweedie's formula (Efron Eq. 1.4), verbatim: "E{μ|z} = z + σ² l′(z) where l′(z) = d/dz log f(z)" — [Efron 2011](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)
- Attribution: "Herbert Robbins (1956) credits personal correspondence with Maurice Kenneth Tweedie for an extraordinary Bayesian estimation formula"; Robbins presents it "as an exponential family generalization" — [Efron 2011 §1–2](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)
- Posterior variance (Efron Eq. 2.8): "μ|z ∼ (z + σ² l′(z), σ²(1 + σ² l″(z)))"; general exponential-family form (Eq. 2.7): `η|z ~ (l'(z) − l0'(z), l''(z) − l0''(z))`; "if f(z) is log concave … then var(η|z) is less than σ²" — [Efron 2011](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)
- Efron's one-line reading: "E{μ|z} = unbiased estimate plus Bayes correction" (Eq. 2.9) — [Efron 2011](https://efron.ckirby.su.domains/papers/2011TweediesFormula.pdf)
- EDM's statement of the same identity in diffusion notation (Eqs. 2–3): minimise `E_{y~p_data} E_{n~N(0,σ²I)} ‖D(y+n; σ) − y‖²₂`, "then ∇_x log p(x; σ) = (D(x; σ) − x)/σ²"; Figure 1(b) shows the "Ideal denoiser outputs D(x; σ)" ("Optimal denoising result from minimizing Eq. 2 analytically … With increasing noise level, the result approaches dataset mean") — [Karras et al. 2022 §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00364)
- Consistency training uses the single-sample version `∇ log p_t(x_t) ≈ −(x_t − x)/t²` as an unbiased estimator — [Song et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01469)
- Anderson, B. D. O., "Reverse-time diffusion equation models", *Stochastic Processes and their Applications* 12(3), 313–326 (1982), DOI 10.1016/0304-4149(82)90051-5; abstract: reverse-time stochastic diffusion models are defined and "most processes defined via a forward-time or conventional diffusion equation model have an associated reverse-time model" — [ANU research portal record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/reverse-time-diffusion-equation-models/) (the ScienceDirect page returned 403; the formula as used in ML is Song et al. Eq. 6 above — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456))
- Vincent, P. (2011), "A connection between score matching and denoising autoencoders", *Neural Computation* 23(7), 1661–1674: shows that the denoising-autoencoder criterion "amounts to matching the data score of an energy-based model with that of a nonparametric Parzen density estimate", i.e. denoising score matching — [copy hosted at gwern.net](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/diffusion/2011-vincent.pdf) (secondary host; publisher page not fetched). Song et al.'s continuous DSM objective (Eq. 7): `θ* = argmin E_t{λ(t) E ‖s_θ(x(t),t) − ∇_{x(t)} log p_{0t}(x(t)|x(0))‖²}`, "building on denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011)" — [Song et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13456)
- Miyasawa, K., "An empirical Bayes estimator of the mean of a normal population", *Bull. Inter. Statist. Inst.* 38, 181–187 — one reference list dates it 1960, most ML citations say 1961 — [Taylor & Francis reference list](https://www.informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/03610927508827311) (bibliographic entry only; paper text not found)

### Inferences
- Translation to diffusion variables. EDM units (`x = x_0 + σε`): `E[x_0 | x] = x + σ² ∇_x log p(x; σ)` — Tweedie with `μ = x_0`, `z = x`. VP/DDPM units (`x_t = sqrt(ᾱ_t) x_0 + sqrt(1−ᾱ_t) ε`): apply Tweedie to `x_t/sqrt(ᾱ_t)`, giving `E[x_0 | x_t] = (x_t + (1−ᾱ_t) ∇ log p_t(x_t)) / sqrt(ᾱ_t)`, equivalently `∇ log p_t(x_t) = −ε̂_θ/sqrt(1−ᾱ_t)`. Rectified-flow units (`z_t = (1−t)x_0 + tε`): `E[x_0 | z_t] = (z_t + t² ∇ log p_t(z_t))/(1−t)`; the SD3 reference code's `calculate_denoised = input − output·σ` is this estimate with the network predicting `v = ε − x_0`. These are my derivations from Efron (1.4) and the forward-process definitions; they are standard but no fetched source prints all three.
- Hence "the network is a denoiser" and "the network estimates the score" are the same statement, exactly, for every family above, because every family's per-level corruption is additive isotropic Gaussian — Tweedie needs nothing else. The paper can say this without qualification.
- Efron's posterior-variance line (`σ²(1 + σ² l''(z))`) gives the *uncertainty* of the denoiser for free from the Hessian of the log-density; log-concavity of the marginal shrinks it below the noise variance.

### Gaps
- Miyasawa 1961 could not be fetched; only the bibliographic entry (with a 1960/1961 discrepancy) was found. If the paper wants to credit the *multivariate* "optimal denoiser = score step" form to Miyasawa rather than to Robbins/Tweedie, that attribution should be checked against the original.
- Vincent 2011 was cited through a secondary host and through Song et al.; the exact DSM equation from Vincent's text was not quoted.
- Anderson 1982: the exact statement of the reverse SDE in Anderson's own notation was not read (paywall); it is cited through Song et al. 2021 and Karras et al. 2022, both of which name it as the source of the time-reversal formula.

## What this means for the paper

The paper can say, truthfully and with citations, that "the noise in Stable Diffusion (1.x, 2.x, SDXL) is Brownian": the forward process is a 1000-step Gaussian Markov chain that is the Euler–Maruyama discretisation of the variance-preserving SDE `dx = −½β(t)x dt + sqrt(β(t)) dw` (Song et al. 2021 Eq. 11, Ho et al. 2020 Eq. 2), i.e. an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process driven by a standard Wiener process, applied to the 4-channel VAE latent rather than to pixels — and the reverse process is Anderson's time-reversed diffusion (in theory) solved as a deterministic ODE (in practice). For SD3 and FLUX the safe wording is: "the forward process is a straight-line interpolation `z_t = (1−t)x_0 + tε` between the latent and a single Gaussian draw — at every noise level the corruption is still additive isotropic Gaussian noise, and the marginals coincide with those of a Brownian (OU-type) diffusion, but the path itself is deterministic and no Wiener increments are ever sampled"; the phrase that stays true across all families is "additive Gaussian noise at every level, with the optimal denoiser given by Tweedie's formula", not "Brownian motion".
