## Q1. Which works state a THEOREM vs an empirical observation? (work-by-work: exact claim, exact evidence)

### Takeaway
No paper in the set states a theorem of the form "the order in which a diffusion model generates is dictated by the data power spectrum". The spectral-ordering claim is everywhere a derivation-plus-plot: Rissanen et al. prove only the trivial additivity E[PSD(u+ε)] = PSD(u) + γ² (their App. A.5, explicitly called "a formal explanation") and then read the ordering off a plot of 1/f^α against a flat noise floor; Dieleman calls it an approximate equivalence "in expectation"; Hoogeboom & Salimans prove an exact equivalence (blurring = Gaussian diffusion with per-frequency schedules) but say nothing about natural-image spectra; Guth et al. prove theorems about the *number of time steps* (condition number κ of the covariance, κ ∼ L^η for power-law spectra) rather than about ordering; Kadkhodaie et al. 2024 give an oracle-shrinkage formula that is the per-coefficient Wiener gain, without naming it. The only text found that writes the power-law Gaussian Wiener filter in closed form inside a diffusion paper and derives a scale-equivariance from it is the Hunt/Kamb/Ganguli 2026 preprint (App. G), and it too stops short of an ordering theorem.

### Cited Findings

**Dieleman 2024, "Diffusion is spectral autoregression" (blog, sander.ai, 2 Sept 2024)**
- Claim, verbatim: "diffusion models of images perform approximate autoregression in the frequency domain!"; scope "at least for visual data"; framed as "a very smooth approximation to the 'hard' autoregression used in e.g. large language models" — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Status: explicitly an empirical, in-expectation observation, not a theorem: holds "in expectation, averaged across many images"; the SNR threshold is "quite arbitrary"; the filtering view is "an approximate equivalence (in expectation) between adding noise to images, and low-pass filtering them" and "this is the case only because of the characteristic shape of natural image spectra" — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Evidence: radially averaged PSD (pysteps RAPSD, green channel, log-domain average) of Imagenette images; same for Gaussian noise (flat) and noisy images at several variances ("rising tide" animation); a "maximal detectable frequency" f_max(t) for rectified flow (σ(t)=t, α(t)=1−t) defined by SNR threshold τ=1, i.e. the condition R[x₀](f) > (t/(1−t))², plotted falling from Nyquist near t=0 to ≈0 near t=1 — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Own fit of the power-law exponent on Imagenette: α̂ = 2.454 (attributed to man-made objects with smooth surfaces and straight edges); states α is "often remarkably close to 2"; cites van der Schaaf & van Hateren 1996, Torralba & Oliva 2003, Hyvärinen, Hurri & Hoyer 2009 — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Counter-example in the same post: for audio waveforms (GTZAN) the average spectrum is neither a power law nor monotonic, so "the 'diffusion is just spectral autoregression' meme does not apply to audio waveforms!" — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Attribution: says Rissanen et al. (§2.2) "directly inspired this blog post"; Wiener filter, Tweedie, Hoogeboom, Kadkhodaie, Guth are not mentioned — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)

**Rissanen, Heinonen, Solin, "Generative Modelling With Inverse Heat Dissipation", ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2206.13397, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2206.13397; PDF header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2023")**
- Claim (§2.2, verbatim): "The power spectral density (PSD) of natural images obeys an approximate power law 1/f^α, where often α ≈ 2 (van der Schaaf & van Hateren, 1996; Hyvärinen et al., 2009)." … "In App. A.5, we show that if we add isotropic Gaussian noise, the PSD of the noise and the PSD of the original image are additive in expectation. Thus, the highest frequencies get drowned out by the noise while the lower frequencies stay intact. … Thus, in the reverse process, the diffusion model generates frequencies starting from the coarse-grained structure and progressing toward fine details." Figure 4 caption: "The 1/f^α power spectral density in natural images induces an implicit coarse-to-fine inductive bias in diffusion models." — [Rissanen et al. 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397)
- The only formal content is App. A.5 (eqs. 52–57): with noise ε of covariance γ²I, E_ε[PSD(u+ε)_i] = PSD(u)_i + γ², "meaning that the PSD of the image and the variance of the noise add together. This gives a formal explanation for the idea that isotropic noise effectively drowns out the frequency components in the data with a lower PSD than the variance of the noise." No Theorem/Proposition environment exists in the paper — [Rissanen et al. 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397)
- Contrast with their own model: "While the frequency content in standard diffusion models is implicitly removed by drowning it out in noise, we do it explicitly by decaying the highest frequencies faster than the lower ones … The noise level σ sets a floor for the frequency components." Heat equation in DCT basis: ũ(t) = exp(Λt)ũ(0) with Λ diagonal, entries −π²(n²/W² + m²/H²); on the infinite plane equals Gaussian blur of variance σ_B² = 2t (citing Bredies & Lorenz 2018) — [Rissanen et al. 2023, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.13397)
- Priority: "Concurrently, the implicit spectral inductive bias was also noted in Kreis et al. (2022)" = Kreis, Gao, Vahdat, CVPR 2022 tutorial on denoising diffusion models — [Rissanen et al. 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397)

**Hoogeboom & Salimans, "Blurring Diffusion Models", ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2209.05557; header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2023")**
- Exact claim (abstract): "we show that blurring can equivalently be defined through a Gaussian diffusion process with non-isotropic noise" — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05557)
- Evidence is an exact derivation, not a theorem environment: with u_t = Vᵀz_t (orthogonal DCT), q(u_t|u_x) = N(u_t | d_t·u_x, σ²I) (eq. 13), d_t = exp(−λτ_t), "Essentially, d_t multiplies higher frequencies with smaller values"; per scalar frequency u_t^(i) = d_t^(i)u_0^(i) + σε (eq. 14) "can be recognized as a special case of the standard Gaussian diffusion process … Rissanen et al. (2022) have chosen α_t = d_t and σ_t² = σ²"; "only the ratio α_t/σ_t matters" (citing Kingma et al. 2021). Markov transitions exist: α_{t|s} = d_t/d_s, σ²_{t|s} = (1 − (d_t/d_s)²)σ² (eq. 16): "if d_t … contains lower values for higher frequencies, then σ_{t|s} will add more noise on the higher frequencies per timestep. The heat dissipation model thus destroys information more quickly for those frequencies as compared to standard diffusion." — [Hoogeboom & Salimans 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05557)
- Inductive-bias statement is qualitative only: "Blurring is a natural destruction for images, because it retains low frequencies over higher frequencies" and "Blurring diffusion places more on emphasis low frequencies which are visually more important, and it may also avoid over-fitting to high frequencies." No mention of 1/f spectra or of standard diffusion already being coarse-to-fine; no Theorem/Proposition in the paper — [Hoogeboom & Salimans 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.05557)

**Guth, Coste, De Bortoli, Mallat, "Wavelet Score-Based Generative Modeling", NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv:2208.05003)**
- Theorems are about the number of time steps, not generation order. Theorem 1 (Gaussian data, uniform step δ, Tr Σ = d, κ = condition number of Σ): KL(p‖p̃₀) ≤ E_T + E_δ + E_{T,δ}, and "for any ε > 0, there exists T, δ ≥ 0 such that (1/d)(E_T + E_δ) ≤ ε and T/δ ≤ Cε⁻²κ³" (eq. 6). Commentary: "For stationary processes of images, the covariance eigenvalues are given by the power spectrum, which typically decays like |ω|⁻¹ at a frequency ω. It results that κ is proportional to a power of the image size." — [Guth et al. 2022, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05003)
- Theorem 2 extends to non-Gaussian p via regularity constants K, M of the score (TV bound with Eδ ∝ √δ[1 + K + M(…)]). Theorem 3 (Gaussian stationary, P(ω) = c(ξ^η + |ω|^η)⁻¹, wavelet with q ≥ η vanishing moments): error ≤ ε with "T/δ ≤ Cε⁻²" — step count independent of dimension, because renormalized wavelet coefficients have dimension-independent condition number (via Meyer 1992). Definition: "We define a multiscale process as a stationary process whose power spectrum has a power law decay … P(ω) ∼ (ξ^η + |ω|^η)⁻¹ … Natural images also typically define stationary processes. Their power spectrum satisfy this property with η = 1 and 2π/ξ ≈ L which is the image width." Numerics: "N(ε) scales like a power of the conditioning number κ of Σ, which, for multiscale gaussian processes, is κ ∼ L^η." — [Guth et al. 2022, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05003)
- Coarse-to-fine here is a *design choice* justified by conditioning: "The inverse generative process is computed from coarse to fine scales as follows"; abstract: "synthesizes wavelet coefficients with the same number of time steps at all scales, and its time complexity therefore grows linearly with the image size. This is proved mathematically over Gaussian distributions" — [Guth et al. 2022, arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05003)

**Kadkhodaie, Guth, Mallat, Simoncelli, "Learning multi-scale local conditional probability models of images", ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2303.02984; header confirms ICLR 2023)**
- No theorem. Coarse-to-fine is an assumption: "the image probability distribution is factorized as a product of conditional probabilities of its wavelet coefficients conditioned by coarser scale coefficients. We assume that these conditional probabilities are local and stationary". Lists diffusion models "(Ho et al., 2022; Guth et al., 2022)" among methods that "use coarse-to-fine strategies". Evidence: denoising/super-resolution/synthesis on faces with conditional RFs as small as 9×9 "without harming performance", vs. pixel-domain CNNs that degrade — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02984)
- Only spectral remark: "the classical Gaussian spectral model for images or sounds rests on an assumption of translation-invariance (stationarity), which guarantees factorization in the Fourier domain." No 1/f, no Wiener, no scale-invariance derivation (a scale-invariance assumption is mentioned once, as a possible weight-sharing restriction) — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2023, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02984)

**Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat, "Generalization in diffusion models arises from geometry-adaptive harmonic representations", ICLR 2024 oral / Outstanding Paper (arXiv:2310.02557)**
- Claim: a bias-free DNN denoiser is f(y) = ∇f(y)y = Σ_k λ_k(y)⟨y,e_k(y)⟩e_k(y) (eq. 6), "performing shrinkage with factors λ_k(y) along axes of a basis specified by e_k(y)" (Jacobian eigendecomposition); observed eigenvectors are "harmonics on the two regions and harmonics along the boundary" (GAHB). Evidence: empirical (Jacobian spectra, PSNR slopes, two networks trained on disjoint halves of CelebA converge to the same denoiser). No theorem environment — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2024, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557)
- The Wiener-type statement, without the word "Wiener": the oracle denoiser in a fixed basis has error E[Σ_k (1−λ_k(x))²⟨x,e_k⟩² + λ_k(x)²σ²] (eq. 11) "which is minimized when λ_k(x) = ⟨x,e_k⟩²/(⟨x,e_k⟩² + σ²). The coefficient λ_k(x) thus acts as a soft threshold: λ_k(x) ≈ 1 when the signal dominates the noise and λ_k(x) ≈ 0 when the signal is weaker than the noise." Oracle error ∼ Σ_k min(⟨x,e_k⟩², σ²) = Mσ² + ‖x − x_M‖², with x_M the M-term approximation using coefficients above the noise level; if ⟨x,e_k⟩² ∼ k^{−(α+1)} the MSE ∼ σ^{2α/(α+1)} (eq. 13), i.e. PSNR slope α/(α+1) — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2024, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557)
- No mention of 1/f or power-law *Fourier* spectra, nor of low-frequency-first generation; the only coarse-to-fine remark is in an appendix on data requirements: "conditioned on coarser content of the image, learning the finer details requires less data due to the conditional Markov property of images (Kadkhodaie et al., 2023)" — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2024, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557)

**Falck et al. 2025, "A Fourier Space Perspective on Diffusion Models" (arXiv:2505.11278, Microsoft Research Cambridge, 16 May 2025)** — not in the assigned list but the most explicit recent statement
- States the mechanism in SNR terms: "The SNR of low frequencies is orders of magnitudes higher than the SNR of high frequencies at all timesteps under a DDPM forward process … a DDPM forward process does not 'noise all frequencies equally'. Relative to the signal, the white noise of the DDPM forward process, which is equal for all frequencies, corrupts high-frequency information faster, i.e. in fewer timesteps." Calls the data property "the Fourier power law" (citing van der Schaaf & van Hateren 1996, Hyvärinen et al. 2009, Rissanen et al. 2022). Evidence: Fig. 1 power laws for CIFAR-10 images, video, audio, cryo-EM maps; an "EqualSNR" forward process that removes the hierarchy and is on par with DDPM on image benchmarks. Formal results (Props. 1–3, Lemma 1) concern SNR/covariance and the Gaussianity of the reverse kernel, not the ordering itself — [Falck et al. 2025, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11278)

**Hunt, Kamb, Ganguli 2026, "An exact information theory of generalization phase transitions in Bayesian diffusion models" (arXiv:2607.08041, 9 Jul 2026; preprint, unreviewed)**
- Writes the Gaussian power-law case explicitly (App. G): "For a power-law Gaussian distribution with power spectral density P(k) = A|k|^{−α}, the Wiener filter in Fourier space is given by ŵ(σ², k) = 1/(1 + A⁻¹σ²|k|^α)" (eq. 198), with the "renormalization condition" ŵ(βσ², k) = ŵ(σ², β^{1/α}k) (eq. 199): "the family of Wiener filters is scale-equivariant: the filter used for a given noise level σ² is simply a rescaled and dilated version of the filter at any other given noise level." Also defines the spectral scale L_spec = (σ_t²/A)^{1/α}, "the inverse of the spatial frequency where the noise power σ_t² equals the signal power", interpreted "as the approximate radius of the optimal linear denoiser, or the Wiener filter [41]" ([41] = Wiener 1949). States "α is typically α ≈ 2 − ε, with ε typically in the range 0.1-0.3" citing Ruderman & Bialek [39]. Theorem 5.2 is about the critical patch scale L_c(σ_t) ∼ O(σ_t√ln|D|), not about ordering — [Hunt et al. 2026, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)
- Secondary report of the lineage: Kamb & Ganguli and Lukoianov et al. "found that image generation proceeds starting from a large coarse spatial scale to a small fine one" (their [32]: Lukoianov, Yuan, Solomon, Sitzmann, "Locality in image diffusion") — [Hunt et al. 2026, arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)

### Inferences
- The chain "flat noise spectrum + power-law signal spectrum ⇒ per-frequency SNR ∝ f^{−α}/σ² ⇒ high frequencies cross SNR = 1 first" is proven in every paper only at the level of E[PSD] additivity (Rissanen A.5) or exact Gaussian-diffusion-in-DCT bookkeeping (Hoogeboom eqs. 13–16). The step "therefore the reverse process *generates* low frequencies first" is asserted, not proved, in all of them; for Gaussian data it would follow from the Wiener gain S(f)/(S(f)+σ²) plus Anderson time reversal, and Hunt et al. 2026 eq. 198–199 is the only diffusion-paper text that writes that gain down.
- Kadkhodaie 2024 eq. (11)–(12) is the Wiener/oracle formula per coefficient (λ_k = s_k²/(s_k²+σ²)), so the "Tweedie/Wiener erasure order" step has a published, if unnamed, form in a basis adapted to the image rather than the Fourier basis.
- Guth et al. frame the same physics (power-law spectrum ⇒ κ ∼ L^η) as a *cost* (ill-conditioning), and their remedy — coarse-to-fine wavelet generation with the same step count per scale — is the only theorem-backed "coarse-to-fine" result, but it is a theorem about *their* algorithm's step count, not about what plain diffusion does.

### Gaps
- Guth et al. write "η = 1" and "decays like |ω|⁻¹" for natural images, which reads as 1/f not 1/f²; whether |ω| there indexes eigenvalues (so that the k-th eigenvalue ∝ 1/k ⇔ 1/|ω|² in 2-D) or radial frequency could not be settled from the PDF. Flag before quoting as a 1/f² citation.
- The NeurIPS 2022 venue of Guth et al. is not printed on the arXiv PDF; it is widely listed as NeurIPS 2022 but was not verified from a primary page here.
- Kreis, Gao, Vahdat CVPR 2022 tutorial (the "concurrent" spectral-bias note) was not retrieved; known only via Rissanen's citation.
- No OpenReview ids were collected for the ICLR papers.

## Q2. What measured slope β do natural images have?

### Takeaway
Power spectra of natural images fall as ≈1/f^β with β ≈ 2 (amplitude ≈ 1/f); the well-measured values are β = 2 − η with η = 0.19 ± 0.01 (Ruderman & Bialek, woodland scenes, ≈1.81), and category means between 1.83 and 2.37 depending on scene type and orientation (Torralba & Oliva, fits on 12 000 images), with per-image standard deviations ≈0.45–0.58.

### Cited Findings
- Simoncelli & Olshausen 2001 (Annu. Rev. Neurosci. 24:1193–1216, DOI 10.1146/annurev.neuro.24.1.1193), verbatim: "many authors have found that the spectral power of natural images falls with frequency, f, according to a power law, 1/f^p, with estimated values for p typically near 2 (see Tolhurst (1992) or Ruderman & Bialek (1994) for reviews)." Explanations listed: scale invariance ("Only a Fourier spectrum that falls as a power law will retain its shape under this transformation"), edges ("edges themselves have a 1/f² power spectrum"), and object size/distance distributions (Ruderman 1997; Lee & Mumford 1999) — [Simoncelli & Olshausen 2001, PDF](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/pub/eero/simoncelli01.pdf)
- Torralba & Oliva 2003 (Network: Comput. Neural Syst. 14:391–412), §2.1 verbatim: "Seminal studies (Burton and Moorhead 1987, Field 1987, 1994, Tolhurst et al 1992) have observed that the average power spectrum of natural images falls with a form 1/f^α with α ∼ 2 (or α ∼ 1 considering the amplitude spectrum, see figure 2(a))." Orientation-dependent model E[|I(f,θ)|²] ≃ A_s(θ)/f^{α_s(θ)} (eq. 1). Database: "about 12 000 pictures of scenes and objects … 256 × 256 pixels" — [Torralba & Oliva 2003, PDF](https://web.mit.edu/torralba/www/ne3302.pdf)
- Torralba & Oliva 2003, Table 1 (fit of A/f^α per image over [0.02, 0.35] cycles/pixel, >3500 images per category, SD in parentheses): Natural — horizontal α = 1.98 (0.58), oblique 2.02 (0.53), vertical 2.22 (0.55); Man-made — horizontal 1.83 (0.58), oblique 2.37 (0.45), vertical 2.07 (0.52). Fig. 3: "The size of the spectral signature is correlated with the slope (α)"; per-category (14 categories) only contour plots, no numeric table — [Torralba & Oliva 2003, PDF](https://web.mit.edu/torralba/www/ne3302.pdf)
- Ruderman & Bialek, "Statistics of natural images: scaling in the woods", NIPS 6 (1993) pp. 551–558, §3.3 verbatim: "If the ensemble is scale-invariant, then the spectrum should be of the form S(k) = A/k^{2−η} … The spectrum … shows that the spectrum scales over about 2.5 decades in spatial frequency. We determine the parameters as A = (6.47±0.13) × 10⁻³ deg^(0.19) and η = 0.19 ± 0.01." (i.e. β = 1.81). - Ruderman 1994 review, "The statistics of natural images", Network 5(4):517–548, DOI 10.1088/0954-898X/5/4/006, abstract: "Perhaps the most notable property of natural images is an invariance to scale" (full text paywalled) — [IOP record](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0954-898X_5_4_006)

- Diffusion-side numbers: Dieleman's Imagenette fit α̂ = 2.454 — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html); Hunt et al. 2026 "α ≈ 2 − ε, with ε typically in the range 0.1-0.3" (citing Ruderman & Bialek) and a synthetic Gaussian test field with α = 1.7 — [Hunt et al. 2026, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041); Rissanen et al. ### Inferences
- "β ≈ 2" is a central tendency, not a constant: the spread across orientations and scene types (1.83–2.37) and the per-image SD (~0.5) are of the same order as the man-made/natural difference, so any argument that needs an *exact* exponent (e.g. an exact Hurst value) must be stated for an ensemble, as Dieleman does ("in expectation").
- η ≈ 0.19 (β ≈ 1.81) in forest scenes vs 2.45 on Imagenette shows the exponent is dataset-dependent; diffusion-model claims inherit that dependence.

- Field 1987 primary text could not be fetched (Optica interstitial; PubMed cookie wall; two PDF mirrors dead), so its own wording and image count are unverified; its page range beyond the first page (2379) is not confirmed here. The β ≈ 2 attribution rests on Torralba & Oliva 2003, Simoncelli & Olshausen 2001 and Ruderman & Bialek 1993 quoting it.
- "Daniel & Tauber" could not be identified as any paper on natural-image statistics; it may be a conflation (Daniel Ruderman?). Treat as non-existent until the user supplies a citation.
- Per-category numeric slopes (beyond natural/man-made × orientation) are not tabulated in Torralba & Oliva 2003; only contour-plot signatures.

## Q3. Does any paper say a single noise schedule works across scales BECAUSE images are scale-invariant?

### Takeaway
No paper found says this in those words. The two theorem-level results that come closest both route through the power-law spectrum: Guth et al. prove that *renormalized wavelet* coefficients of a power-law Gaussian process can be sampled with the same number of steps at every scale (Theorem 3), and Hunt et al. 2026 prove the power-law Wiener filter family is scale-equivariant (noise level ↔ dilation). The pixel-domain empirical literature says the opposite for raw resolution changes: the schedule must be shifted in log-SNR with resolution (simple diffusion; Chen 2023), which the pooling argument explains without invoking scale invariance.

### Cited Findings
- Guth et al. 2022 abstract: WSGM "synthesizes wavelet coefficients with the same number of time steps at all scales … This is proved mathematically over Gaussian distributions"; Theorem 3 is stated for P(ω) = c(ξ^η + |ω|^η)⁻¹ and gives T/δ ≤ Cε⁻² independent of dimension; §3.2: "the normalization of wavelet coefficients performs a preconditioning of the covariance, whose eigenvalues then remain of the order of 1" (Meyer 1992) — [Guth et al. 2022, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05003)
- Hunt et al. 2026 eq. 199: ŵ(βσ², k) = ŵ(σ², β^{1/α}k); real space w(βσ², x) = β^{−2/α}w(σ², β^{−1/α}x): "the filter used for a given noise level σ² is simply a rescaled and dilated version of the filter at any other given noise level" (power-law Gaussian data only) — [Hunt et al. 2026, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)
- Hoogeboom, Heek, Salimans, "simple diffusion" (ICML 2023, arXiv:2301.11093): average-pooling a 2×2 block of z_t gives z_t^{64×64} ∼ N(α_t x^{64×64}, σ_t/2): "The lower resolution pixel z_t^{64×64} only has half the amount of noise. We hypothesize that as resolutions increase this is problematic, as much less diffusion time is spent on the lower resolution, a stage at which the global consistency is generated." Remedy: shift the schedule with resolution — [simple diffusion, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11093)
- Chen 2023, "On the importance of noise scheduling for diffusion models" (arXiv:2301.10972): "the optimal noise scheduling … shifts towards a noisier one (due to increased redundancy in pixels)"; "scaling the input data by a factor of b while keeping the noise schedule function fixed (equivalent to shifting the logSNR by log b) is a good strategy across image sizes" — [Chen 2023, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10972)
- Dieleman's f_max(t) construction maps each noise level to a cutoff frequency using the measured power law (threshold R[x₀](f) > (t/(1−t))²) — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Falck et al. 2025 take the converse position: an "EqualSNR" forward process that "corrupts all frequencies at the same rate" removes the hierarchy and "performs on par with DDPM on standard imaging benchmarks" — [Falck et al. 2025, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11278)

### Inferences
- The consistent reading is: on power-law data, noise level and spatial scale are the same coordinate (σ² ↔ |k|^α by eq. 199; cutoff f ∝ σ^{−2/α}), so one schedule *in the scale-relative variable* (per-scale wavelet SNR, or log-SNR shifted by 2 log s per pooling factor s) is justified by the spectrum, while one schedule *in raw pixel SNR* is not. Nobody in the set states this synthesis; it has to be made in the paper.
- Falck et al.'s EqualSNR result is evidence that the coarse-to-fine hierarchy is a consequence of the forward process meeting the spectrum, not a requirement for good samples.

### Gaps
- Whether simple diffusion's authors attribute the shift to natural-image statistics (as opposed to pooling arithmetic) was not checked beyond the quoted passage; the "2 log s" log-SNR formula appears only in a 2026 secondary source ("Fractals made Practical", arXiv:2603.13069) and was not verified in the primary.
- Hunt et al. 2026 is a two-month-old unreviewed preprint.

## Q4. Exact statement of "diffusion is spectral autoregression" and the supporting experiment

### Takeaway
Dieleman's statement is that image diffusion models "perform approximate autoregression in the frequency domain": because natural-image RAPSDs are power laws and Gaussian noise is flat, adding noise of variance σ² is, in expectation, equivalent to low-pass filtering at the frequency where the signal PSD crosses σ², so the reverse process fills in frequencies from low to high. The support is a measured RAPSD on Imagenette (fit α̂ = 2.454), RAPSDs of noisy images at increasing variance showing a hinge that moves to lower frequencies, and a plotted f_max(t) for rectified flow; the author marks it as approximate, in-expectation, and image-specific (fails for audio waveforms).

### Cited Findings
- Statement and scope: "diffusion models of images perform approximate autoregression in the frequency domain!" (visual data; "a very smooth approximation" to LLM-style autoregression) — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Experiment: Imagenette images, green channel, 2-D magnitude/phase spectra; RAPSD via pysteps averaged in the log domain over a set of images; RAPSD of standard Gaussian noise (flat); RAPSDs of noisy images at several variances including 16 (hinge, "rising tide" animation); f_max(t) under σ(t)=t, α(t)=1−t with SNR threshold τ=1 → condition R[x₀](f) > (t/(1−t))², plotted from Nyquist (t≈0) to ≈0 (t≈1); audio control on GTZAN (not a power law, non-monotonic) — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Caveats stated by the author: per-image spectra are not straight lines and "will not typically be monotonically decreasing"; the threshold is "quite arbitrary"; the transition zone between signal and noise is wide; the filtering equivalence is "in expectation" and holds "only because of the characteristic shape of natural image spectra" — [Dieleman 2024](https://sander.ai/2024/09/02/spectral-autoregression.html)
- Antecedent: Rissanen et al. §2.2 and Fig. 4 (PSD of noised images, "coarse-grained structure … toward fine details"), App. A.5 additivity — [Rissanen et al. 2023, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397); Falck et al. 2025 cite Kingma & Gao 2024 and Dieleman 2024 for "approximately autoregressive" generation in DDPM — [Falck et al. 2025, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11278)

### Inferences
- The blog's argument is exactly the first three steps of the four-step chain (flat noise → power-law signal → crossover frequency) with the fourth step (reverse process order) taken as given from how diffusion sampling works; it is empirical about the spectrum and definitional about the rest.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measurement of the *reverse* process's spectral content vs. step appears in the blog; the evidence is about the forward process in expectation.

## Q5. The Gaussian-field / Wiener case stated in the diffusion context, and fBm spectra citations

### Takeaway
The Wiener gain S(f)/(S(f)+σ²) for Gaussian signal plus white noise is written down inside diffusion papers in three places: Kadkhodaie 2024 eq. (11)–(12) (per coefficient, "soft threshold", not named Wiener), Hunt et al. 2026 eq. 198 (named, power-law closed form, scale-equivariance), and Rissanen App. A.5 (the additive PSD, which is the denominator of the gain). For fBm the 1-D spectral exponent 2H+1 is due to Mandelbrot & Van Ness (proportionality argument) and Flandrin 1989 (time-averaged Wigner–Ville spectrum); the 2-D surface exponent 2H+2 follows from β = d + 2H, found only in secondary sources here.

### Cited Findings
- Kadkhodaie et al. 2024: oracle shrinkage λ_k(x) = ⟨x,e_k⟩²/(⟨x,e_k⟩² + σ²), "≈ 1 when the signal dominates the noise and ≈ 0 when the signal is weaker than the noise"; error ∼ Σ_k min(⟨x,e_k⟩², σ²) — [Kadkhodaie et al. 2024, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02557)
- Hunt et al. 2026: ŵ(σ², k) = 1/(1 + A⁻¹σ²|k|^α) for P(k) = A|k|^{−α}; normalized, → 0 as k → ∞; scale-equivariant (eq. 199–200); L_spec = (σ_t²/A)^{1/α} as the radius of the Wiener filter; sampling by reversing the forward process via "Tweedie's theorem" — [Hunt et al. 2026, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08041)
- Rissanen et al. App. A.5: E_ε[PSD(u+ε)_i] = PSD(u)_i + γ² — [Rissanen et al. 2023, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13397)
- Other diffusion-context Wiener derivations surfaced by search (not read in full): a CVPR 2023 supplement gives H*(f) = σ²/(|X(f)|² + σ²)-type WSS results — [Yang et al. CVPR 2023 supp.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Yang_Diffusion_Probabilistic_Model_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf); a 2025 remote-sensing paper writes the DDPM denoiser as ᾱ_t/(ᾱ_t + (1−ᾱ_t)f²) under a 1/f² prior — [arXiv:2507.01573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01573); "Ambient Diffusion Policy" bounds W(f) = C/(C + σ²f^α) with |W′(f)| ≤ α/(4f) — [arXiv:2606.12365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12365)
- fBm 1-D: spectral density ∝ |ω|^{−(2H+1)} suggested by Mandelbrot & Van Ness 1968; made rigorous as a time-averaged Wigner–Ville spectrum by P. Flandrin, "On the spectrum of fractional Brownian motions", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 35(1):197–199, Jan 1989; explicit constant σ²Γ(2H+1)sin(πH)/(2πf)^{2H+1} in later reviews; increments (fGn) have exponent 2H−1 — [arXiv:2410.14205](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14205); [Flandrin lecture notes](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/patrick.flandrin/lns95.pdf); [MIT 1991 report citing Flandrin 1989](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/3214/1/P-1991-22439474.pdf)
- fBm d-D: S_H ∝ ‖ω‖^{−β} with β = d + 2H (so 2H+2 for surfaces) — [arXiv:0711.2892](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.2892); tabulated 2-D fBm β = 2H+2 — [arXiv:1107.5287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.5287); Voss's profile relation β = 2H+1 (1 < β < 3) and D = E + (3−β)/2, with the caveat that 2-D-Fourier β and profile β differ — [Symmetry 2016 (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/8/7/66/html); Kube & Pentland: a Lambertian fractal surface with surface spectrum ∝ f^β images with spectrum ∝ f^{2−β} — [MIT Media Lab record](https://www-prod.media.mit.edu/publications/on-the-imaging-of-fractal-surfaces-2)

### Inferences
- Matching 1/f² image statistics to a 2-D fBm surface β = 2H+2 gives H = 0 (the boundary of the fBm range), and to a 1-D profile β = 2H+1 gives H = 1/2 (ordinary Brownian profiles); the paper should say which object (profile vs surface, intensity vs height) it is identifying, since Kube & Pentland show image and surface exponents differ under shading.

### Gaps
- Mandelbrot & Van Ness 1968 (SIAM Review 10:422–437) and Voss 1988 ("Fractals in nature", in Peitgen & Saupe, The Science of Fractal Images) were not opened; the 2H+2 attribution to Voss/Pentland is secondary.
- No paper was found that proves, for a Gaussian field with spectrum S(f), that the *reverse SDE* recovers frequencies in order of S(f)/σ_t²; the Gaussian reverse process is linear (Pierret & Galerne 2024; Biroli et al. 2024 are candidates for an exact-solution citation) but these were not retrieved here.

## What this means for the paper

### Takeaway
The literature supports stating the four-step chain as a theorem for Gaussian/fBm fields and as an empirical regularity for photographs: (1) white noise has a flat spectrum (trivial; Dieleman, Rissanen A.5); (2) the signal has a power-law spectrum — a theorem for fBm (Flandrin 1989: f^{−(2H+1)}; β = d + 2H in d dimensions) and a measurement for images (β ≈ 2: Field 1987 via Torralba & Oliva/Simoncelli & Olshausen; 1.81 Ruderman & Bialek; 1.83–2.37 by scene type); (3) the posterior-mean denoiser is the Wiener gain S/(S+σ²), so modes with S(f) < σ² are erased in order of decreasing frequency — exact for Gaussian fields (Kadkhodaie 2024 eq. 11–12; Hunt et al. 2026 eq. 198, with scale-equivariance 199); (4) Anderson time reversal of that forward erasure yields coarse-to-fine generation. Step 2 is the only step that is merely empirical for photographs, and step 4 is asserted rather than proved by everyone in the set (Rissanen, Dieleman, Falck), so the paper's contribution is to write steps 3–4 out as the theorem the diffusion literature keeps gesturing at, and to say plainly that for photographs the exponent is an ensemble average with spread ≈ 0.5 and dataset dependence (1.81 forest, 2.45 Imagenette).

### Cited Findings
- See Q1–Q5 above; the load-bearing primary quotations are Rissanen A.5 eq. 57, Hoogeboom eqs. 13–16, Guth Theorem 1/3 and eq. 10, Kadkhodaie 2024 eqs. 11–13, Hunt eqs. 198–199, Ruderman & Bialek η = 0.19 ± 0.01, Torralba & Oliva Table 1.

### Inferences
- Cite Rissanen et al. 2023 §2.2/App. A.5 and Kreis et al. 2022 as the origin of the spectral-bias observation, Dieleman 2024 for the "spectral autoregression" phrasing, Falck et al. 2025 for the SNR-hierarchy framing and the EqualSNR counterfactual, Guth et al. 2022 for the theorem that the same spectrum makes pixel-space diffusion ill-conditioned (κ ∼ L^η), and Hunt et al. 2026 for the closed-form power-law Wiener filter — then state the ordering theorem yourself, since none of them does.

### Gaps
- Field 1987 primary wording; Guth's η = 1 convention; a primary 2-D fBm spectrum citation (Voss 1988 / Pentland 1984); an exact-Gaussian reverse-SDE citation (Pierret & Galerne 2024 or Biroli et al. 2024) if the paper wants to lean on prior work for step 4 rather than prove it.
